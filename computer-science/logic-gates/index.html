📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Inside a computer, decisions are made by logic gates: tiny circuits that take one or two inputs, each 0 (off, false) or 1 (on, true), and give an output of 0 or 1. The three basic gates are AND (1 only if all inputs are 1), OR (1 if any input is 1) and NOT (flips its input). Putting NOT after AND or OR gives NAND and NOR, and XOR (exclusive OR) gives 1 only when its inputs are different. A truth table lists the output for every possible combination of inputs, so it describes a gate or circuit completely. Joining gates builds bigger circuits; millions of them make adders, memory and whole processors.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

AND: Y = A · B OR: Y = A + B NOT: Y = A̅ NAND: Y = (A · B)̅ NOR: Y = (A + B)̅ XOR: Y = A ⊕ B n inputs → 2ⁿ rows in the truth table
  • In Boolean algebra "·" means AND and "+" means OR. Here 1 + 1 = 1, because "true OR true" is still just true.
  • De Morgan's laws: (A · B)̅ = A̅ + B̅ and (A + B)̅ = A̅ · B̅. Check them by comparing truth tables.
  • NAND and NOR are universal gates: any circuit at all can be built from NAND gates alone (or NOR gates alone).

Simplification: the gates switch instantly here. Real gates are made of transistors, take a few billionths of a second to switch, and use voltages (for example about 0 V for 0 and a few volts for 1).

🌍 In real life

  • A washing machine starts only if the lid is closed AND the start button is pressed.
  • A two-way staircase switch lets you turn the light on or off from either floor: it behaves like XOR.
  • A half adder, the circuit that adds two bits, uses XOR for the sum bit and AND for the carry bit.

📖 Key terms

  • Logic gate: a circuit that gives a 0 or 1 output from its 0/1 inputs by a fixed rule.
  • Truth table: a table of the output for every combination of inputs.
  • Boolean expression: the gate's rule written with ·, + and bars, named after George Boole.
  • Universal gate: a gate (NAND or NOR) from which all other gates can be built.
