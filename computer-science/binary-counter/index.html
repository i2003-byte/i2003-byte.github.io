📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Computers store everything as bits: tiny switches that are either off (0) or on (1). A row of bits makes a binary (base 2) number. It works just like our decimal (base 10) numbers, except that each place is worth 2 times the place on its right instead of 10 times. So the places of an 8-bit number are worth 128, 64, 32, 16, 8, 4, 2 and 1. To read a binary number, add the place values of the bits that are 1. When you count up, the rightmost bit flips each time; when a 1 becomes 0 it carries into the next place, just like 9 + 1 = 10 in decimal. Because long rows of 0s and 1s are hard for people to read, programmers group bits into octal (groups of 3 bits, base 8) or hexadecimal (groups of 4 bits, base 16, digits 0–9 and A–F).

🌍 In real life

  • One byte (8 bits) can store one English letter: "A" is stored as 65, which is 01000001.
  • Colours on a web page are written in hexadecimal: #FF9933 (the saffron of the flag) means red 255, green 153, blue 51.
  • An old 8-bit video game score counter that reached 255 would suddenly jump back to 0: an overflow.

🎯 Try this

  1. Switch on only the lamps worth 64, 32 and 4. What number do you get? What is it in hexadecimal?
    Show answer64 + 32 + 4 = 100. In binary it is 01100100; the groups 0110 and 0100 give 6 and 4, so it is 64₁₆. In octal it is 144₈.
  2. Set the value to 7 and press +1. How many bits change? Why?
    Show answerFour bits change: 00000111 becomes 00001000. The three 1s on the right each flip to 0 and carry, and the carry finally lands in the 8s place. This is just like 999 + 1 = 1000 in decimal.
  3. Choose 4 bits and count up from 13. What happens after 15?
    Show answer15 is 1111, the largest 4-bit number. Adding 1 needs a fifth bit, which does not exist, so the counter overflows back to 0000.
  4. Look at the rightmost bit while counting. What does it tell you about the number?
    Show answerIt is 1 for odd numbers and 0 for even numbers, because every other place value is even.
  5. Press "New challenge" a few times and make each number. Which lamp should you decide first?
    Show answerStart with the biggest place value that fits, switch it on, subtract it, and repeat with what is left. For 45: 32 fits (13 left), then 8 (5 left), then 4 (1 left), then 1, giving 00101101.

📖 Key terms

  • Bit: a binary digit, 0 or 1. Nibble: 4 bits. Byte: 8 bits.
  • Base (radix): how many different digits a number system uses: 2, 8, 10 or 16.
  • Most significant bit (MSB): the leftmost bit, with the largest place value.
  • Overflow: a result too big to fit in the bits available.
