📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Supply is the amount sellers are willing to sell at each price. The law of supply says that, other things staying the same, a higher price makes sellers offer more, so the supply curve slopes up. Put the supply curve on the same graph as the demand curve and they cross at one point, the equilibrium. At that price, buyers want exactly what sellers offer, so nothing pushes the price up or down. At any other price there is a gap. If the price is too low there is a shortage (excess demand): buyers compete and the price rises. If it is too high there is a surplus (excess supply): unsold mangoes pile up and sellers cut the price. Either way the market moves towards equilibrium.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

Qd = 400 − 2P (+ 80 in a festival week) Qs = 3P − 50 − 3(c − 10) + harvest

Q in kg per day, P = price (₹/kg), c = cost of transport and labour (₹/kg), harvest = +90 bumper crop, −120 after a cyclone

Equilibrium: Qd = Qs → 400 − 2P = 3P − 50 → P* = ₹90, Q* = 220 kg shortage = Qd − Qs (price below P*) · surplus = Qs − Qd (price above P*)
  • A change in costs, weather, technology or the number of sellers shifts the supply curve. Higher costs shift it up (left): sellers now want a higher price for each kilogram.
  • When a curve shifts, the old equilibrium becomes a shortage or a surplus, and the price moves to the new crossing point.

Simplification: straight-line curves and invented round numbers. The speed of the price change (it moves faster when the gap is bigger) is a simple model too; real markets can adjust quickly or slowly, and prices may be sticky.

🌍 In real life

  • After a cyclone hits the orchards of coastal Andhra Pradesh or Odisha, fewer mangoes reach the mandi and prices jump: supply shifted left.
  • When diesel becomes dearer, trucking fruit and vegetables costs more, and prices across the country creep up.
  • In a bumper onion year, prices in Lasalgaon can crash so low that farmers leave onions unsold: supply shifted right.

    Show answerNo. The new equilibrium is ₹102 and 196 kg, a rise of only ₹12. Buyers pay part of the extra cost and sellers absorb the rest, because at a higher price buyers buy less.
  5. Pick a festival week and a bumper crop together. Which way does the price move?
    Show answerBoth curves shift right. The quantity rises a lot (to 304 kg), but the price barely changes (₹88), because the demand push up and the supply push down almost cancel.

📖 Key terms

  • Law of supply: other things being equal, quantity supplied rises as price rises.
  • Equilibrium: the price at which quantity demanded equals quantity supplied.
  • Excess demand (shortage): Qd > Qs at the current price.
  • Excess supply (surplus): Qs > Qd at the current price.
