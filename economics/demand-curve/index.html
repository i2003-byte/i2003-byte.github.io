📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Demand is the amount of a good that buyers are willing and able to buy at each price. A table of prices and quantities is a demand schedule, and its graph is the demand curve. The law of demand says that, other things staying the same, when the price rises the quantity demanded falls, so the curve slopes down. Be careful with two different changes. A change in the price of the good itself moves you along the curve: a change in quantity demanded. A change in anything else (income, prices of related goods, tastes, number of buyers) shifts the whole curve: a change in demand.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

Qd = 400 − 2P + 3(I − 30) + 0.8(Pa − 150) + tastes

Qd in kg per day, P = price of mangoes (₹/kg), I = income (₹ thousand per month), Pa = price of apples (₹/kg)

spending (total expenditure) = P × Q price elasticity e = (ΔQ ÷ ΔP) × (P ÷ Q) = −2 × P ÷ Q
  • If |e| > 1 demand is elastic: a price rise cuts spending. If |e| < 1 it is inelastic: a price rise increases spending. At |e| = 1 spending is at its largest.
  • Normal good: demand rises when income rises (mangoes here). Substitute: when apples get dearer, people buy more mangoes instead.

Simplification: this is a made-up market with a straight-line demand curve and round numbers chosen to be easy to read. Real demand curves are measured from data and are usually curved. Economists draw price on the vertical axis, as here.

🌍 In real life

  • When tomato prices jump in the monsoon, families buy fewer tomatoes and use more tamarind or curd for sourness: a move along the tomato demand curve.
  • At Diwali, demand for sweets and new clothes rises at every price: the whole curve shifts right.
  • Salt has very inelastic demand: even if its price doubled, people would buy almost the same amount.

🎯 Try this

  1. Raise the price from ₹60 to ₹120 per kg. What happens to the quantity demanded? Does the curve move?
    Show answerQuantity demanded falls from 280 kg to 160 kg per day. The curve does not move; the point slides up along it. This is a change in quantity demanded.
  2. Now keep the price at ₹100 and raise income to ₹50 000 a month. What happens?
    Show answerThe whole curve shifts right by 60 kg: at ₹100 buyers now want 260 kg instead of 200 kg. This is an increase in demand, because mangoes are a normal good.
  3. Go back to the starting market. At which price is spending largest? What is the elasticity there?
    Show answerAt ₹100 per kg: 200 kg × ₹100 = ₹20 000 per day. The elasticity there is −1 (unit elastic). Above ₹100 demand is elastic and spending falls; below it demand is inelastic.
  4. Make apples cheaper (₹70 per kg). Which way does the mango curve shift, and why?
    Show answerLeft, by 64 kg at every price. Apples are a substitute for mangoes, so when apples get cheaper some buyers switch to apples.

📖 Key terms

  • Demand schedule: a table showing the quantity demanded at each price.
  • Law of demand: other things being equal, quantity demanded falls as price rises.
  • Substitute: a good that can be used in place of another (tea and coffee, mangoes and apples).
  • Price elasticity of demand: how strongly the quantity demanded responds to a change in price.
