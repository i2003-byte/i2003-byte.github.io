📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

In science, work is done only when a force moves an object, and only the part of the force along the direction of motion counts. Here a box moves along the floor and one force F acts on it at an angle θ. The force is split into two parts: F cos θ along the motion (this does the work) and F sin θ across the motion (this does no work at all). When the force helps the motion the work is positive; when it is at right angles to the motion it is zero; when it acts against the motion, like friction or brakes, the work is negative. On the chart, work is the shaded area under the force–distance line.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

W = F × s × cos θ (1 joule = 1 newton × 1 metre) θ = 0°: W = F s θ = 90°: W = 0 θ = 180°: W = −F s 40 N at 60° over 5 m: W = 40 × 5 × 0.5 = 100 J
  • For Class 9, where the force and the motion are along the same line, this is simply W = F × s.
  • cos 0° = 1, cos 60° = 0.5, cos 90° = 0, cos 180° = −1.
  • Work is a scalar: it has a size and a sign, but no direction.
  • Work done = area under the force–displacement graph.

Simplification: the box moves at a steady 1 m/s and we only work out the work done by this one force. Whatever else keeps the box moving (another push, or its own motion) is left out of the picture.

🌍 In real life

  • Pulling a trolley bag at a railway station by a slanted handle: part of your pull only lifts the handle, so you do less work per metre than the full pull suggests.
  • A coolie (porter) carrying a heavy load on the head while walking on level ground does no work on the load in the scientific sense: the upward supporting force is at 90° to the motion. (The person still gets tired, because muscles use energy just to hold the load.)
  • Friction on a sliding carrom striker does negative work, which is why the striker slows down and stops.
  • Pushing hard against a wall that does not move does no work on the wall, because the displacement is zero.

🎯 Try this

  1. With F = 40 N and s = 5 m, compare θ = 0° and θ = 60°.
    Show answer200 J at 0° and 100 J at 60°, because cos 60° = 0.5. Only half the pull is along the motion.
  2. Press "Porter holding up a 200 N load, walking". Why is the work zero even though the force is big?
    Show answerThe force is straight up and the motion is sideways, so θ = 90° and cos θ = 0. There is no part of the force along the motion.
  3. Press "Friction on a sliding box". What is the sign of the work, and what does that mean?
    Show answerW = 30 × 4 × (−1) = −120 J. Negative work means the force takes energy away from the moving box.
  4. At what angle does a 100 N force do the same work over 5 m as a 50 N force pulling straight along?
    Show answer50 × 5 = 250 J. So 100 × 5 × cos θ = 250 gives cos θ = 0.5, θ = 60°.
  5. A 40 kg box is lifted straight up through 1.5 m at a steady speed. How much work does the lifting force do?
    Show answerThe force equals the weight, 40 × 9.8 = 392 N, and it is along the motion, so W = 392 × 1.5 ≈ 588 J.

📖 Key terms

  • Work: the product of a force and the distance moved along the direction of the force.
  • Joule (J): the work done when a force of 1 N moves an object through 1 m along the force.
  • Positive / negative work: the force acts with / against the direction of motion.
  • Component of a force: the part of a force that acts along a chosen direction.
