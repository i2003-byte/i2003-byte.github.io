📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

The amplitude is how far a vibrating object (or air particle) moves from its resting position. A bigger amplitude pushes the air harder, giving a louder sound. On a wave diagram, amplitude is the height of a crest above the middle line. Loudness grows with the square of the amplitude, so doubling the amplitude makes the sound four times louder.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

Loudness ∝ (amplitude)²

double the amplitude → 4× louder

Loudness is measured in decibels (dB)

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Hitting a drum harder makes a louder sound
  • Turning up the volume makes a speaker cone move further
  • Shouting pushes more air from the lungs than whispering

🎯 Try this

  1. Set the amplitude to 25%, then to 50%. How many times louder is the sound?
    Show answer4 times louder (2² = 4).
  2. Does changing the amplitude change the pitch?
    Show answerNo — the frequency (pitch) stays the same; only loudness changes.
  3. Watch the air particles. What changes when the sound is louder?
    Show answerThey move further back and forth — the compressions are more squeezed.

📖 Key terms

  • Amplitude — The maximum distance a vibrating particle moves from its resting position.
  • Loudness — How strong a sound seems; depends on amplitude.
  • Compression / rarefaction — Regions where air particles are squeezed together / spread apart.
