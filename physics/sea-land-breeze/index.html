📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

In coastal areas, the land heats up faster than the water during the day. The air over the land gets hotter and rises, and cooler air from the sea rushes in to take its place — a sea breeze. At night the land cools down faster than the water, so the air above the sea is now warmer. It rises, and cool air from the land flows towards the sea — a land breeze. Both are convection currents in air.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

Land heats up and cools down faster than water Day: sea → land (sea breeze) Night: land → sea (land breeze) Wind blows from the cooler area to the warmer area

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Coastal towns stay cooler on summer afternoons thanks to the sea breeze
  • Fishermen traditionally sailed out at dawn with the land breeze and came home with the sea breeze
  • Kites fly easily on a beach in the afternoon

🎯 Try this

  1. Set the time to 3 pm. Which way is the wind blowing?
    Show answerFrom the sea to the land — a sea breeze.
  2. Set the time to 3 am. What has changed?
    Show answerThe land is now cooler than the sea, so the wind blows from land to sea — a land breeze.
  3. Look at the graph. Which temperature changes more during the day?
    Show answerThe land — its temperature swings much more than the sea’s.
  4. Why is there hardly any wind around 8 am and again around 7:30 pm?
    Show answerLand and sea are at almost the same temperature, so the air barely moves while the breeze changes direction.

📖 Key terms

  • Sea breeze — Cool wind blowing from the sea to the land during the day.
  • Land breeze — Cool wind blowing from the land to the sea at night.
  • Wind — Moving air, caused by differences in temperature.
