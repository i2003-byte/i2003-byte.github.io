📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

A lens is a piece of glass with curved faces. A convex lens is thicker in the middle and bends parallel rays together to meet at its principal focus; a concave lens is thinner in the middle and spreads them out as if they came from a focus on the incoming side. A lens has a focus on each side, F₁ and F₂, at the same distance f from the optical centre O. Drag the object and follow the rays: where they meet is a real image you can catch on a screen; if they only seem to meet on the object's side, the image is virtual.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

1/v − 1/u = 1/f m = h′ ÷ h = v ÷ u P = 1 ÷ f (f in metres) Convex, f = +15 cm, u = −40 cm: 1/v = 1/15 − 1/40 → v = +24 cm, m = −0.6
  • Sign convention (New Cartesian): measure from O; distances in the direction of the light (to the right) are +, against it are −; heights above the axis are +. So u is negative, f is + for a convex lens and − for a concave lens, and + v means a real image on the far side.
  • Power is measured in dioptres (D): a convex lens of f = 0.25 m has P = +4 D; a concave lens of f = −0.5 m has P = −2 D.
  • The three useful rays: parallel to the axis → through F₂ (or seems to come from F₁ for a concave lens); through O → straight on; through F₁ (or heading for F₂) → comes out parallel.

Images formed by a convex lens

  • Object at infinity → tiny point image at F₂, real and inverted.
  • Beyond 2F₁ → between F₂ and 2F₂: diminished, real, inverted.
  • At 2F₁ → at 2F₂: same size, real, inverted.
  • Between F₁ and 2F₁ → beyond 2F₂: enlarged, real, inverted.
  • At F₁ → at infinity: highly enlarged.
  • Between F₁ and O → on the same side as the object: enlarged, virtual, erect.
  • A concave lens always gives a virtual, erect, diminished image between F₁ and O.

Simplification: this is a thin lens, so the rays are drawn bending once at the line through O (exact only for rays close to the axis), and heights are stretched compared with distances.

🌍 In real life

  • Camera and your eye: a far object gives a small, inverted, real image on the film, sensor or retina.
  • Projector: the slide sits just beyond F, so a big real image falls on a far screen (the slide is put in upside down).
  • Magnifying glass: hold the lens so that the page is inside F to see an enlarged, upright, virtual image.
  • Spectacles: convex lenses for long sight and reading glasses, concave lenses for short sight. Opticians write their power in dioptres, like −1.5 D.
  • Door peepholes use a concave lens to show a small, upright, wide view of the corridor.

🎯 Try this

  1. Convex lens, f = 15 cm, object 40 cm away. Find v and m.
    Show answer1/v = 1/f + 1/u = 1/15 − 1/40 = 5/120, so v = +24 cm (real, on the other side). m = v/u = 24 ÷ (−40) = −0.6: inverted and smaller.
  2. Press "At 2F₁". What do you notice?
    Show answerThe image is at 2F₂, the same size as the object (m = −1), real and inverted. This is used in photocopiers.
  3. Convex lens, f = 10 cm, object 5 cm away. Where is the image and how big is it?
    Show answer1/v = 1/10 − 1/5 = −1/10, so v = −10 cm: on the object's side, virtual. m = −10 ÷ (−5) = +2: upright and twice as tall. A magnifying glass.
  4. Switch to a concave lens and drag the object from far to near. Does the image ever become real?
    Show answerNo. It is always virtual, erect and smaller, and stays between F₁ and O.
  5. What is the power of a concave lens of focal length 25 cm?
    Show answerf = −0.25 m, so P = 1 ÷ (−0.25) = −4 D.

📖 Key terms

  • Optical centre (O): the centre of the lens; a ray through it is not bent.
  • Principal focus (F₁, F₂) and focal length (f): where rays parallel to the axis meet, or seem to come from; f = OF.
  • 2F: the point at twice the focal length, where object and image are the same size.
  • Power (P): how strongly a lens bends light, 1/f in dioptres (D).
  • Magnification (m): image height ÷ object height = v ÷ u.
