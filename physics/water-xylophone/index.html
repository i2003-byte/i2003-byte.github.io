📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

The jal tarang is an Indian instrument played by tapping bowls of water. When you tap a glass, the glass and water vibrate — more water makes the vibration slower, so the pitch gets lower. When you blow across a bottle, the air column above the water vibrates — more water leaves a shorter air column, so the pitch gets higher!

🧮 Key formulas & rules

Tapping: more water → lower pitch

the vibrating glass + water gets heavier

Blowing: f ≈ v ÷ (4 × length of air column)

more water → shorter air column → higher pitch

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Jal tarang in Indian classical music
  • Blowing across a bottle to make a hooting sound
  • Flutes and organ pipes — shorter pipes make higher notes

📖 Key terms

  • Jal tarang — An instrument of water-filled bowls that are struck to make music.
  • Air column — The column of air inside a tube or bottle that vibrates to make sound.
