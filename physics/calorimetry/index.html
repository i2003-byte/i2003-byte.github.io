📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

When a hot body and a cold body touch, heat flows from the hotter one to the colder one until both reach the same temperature (thermal equilibrium). If no heat escapes to the surroundings, the heat lost by the hot body is exactly the heat gained by the cold one. This is the principle of calorimetry, and using it to find an unknown is called the method of mixtures.

Different substances need different amounts of heat to warm up. The specific heat capacity c is the heat needed to raise 1 g (or 1 kg) by 1 °C. Water's is very large (4.18 J/g°C), about 11 times copper's, so a hot copper block hardly warms a cup of water, while the same mass of hot water warms it a lot.

Melting needs heat too, without any rise in temperature: ice at 0 °C takes 334 J per gram (the latent heat of fusion) just to turn into water at 0 °C. That is why ice cools a drink so well.

🧮 Key formulas

Q = m × c × ΔT heat lost by hot body = heat gained by cold body T_final = (m₁c₁T₁ + m₂c₂T₂) ÷ (m₁c₁ + m₂c₂)

Example: 200 g of copper (c = 0.385) at 100 °C into 200 g of water at 25 °C in a 100 g copper can (38.5 J/°C): T = (77 × 100 + 874.5 × 25) ÷ (77 + 874.5) ≈ 31.1 °C.

c of a solid = (m_w c_w + C_can)(T_f − T_w) ÷ (m (T − T_f)) Q to melt = m × L, L(ice) = 334 J/g

How the model works: the calorimeter is perfectly insulated (no heat is lost to the air), the water is always well stirred, and the stirrer and thermometer take no heat. The can and the water are always at the same temperature. Heat flows at a rate proportional to the temperature difference, speeded up so things settle in a few seconds. Ice is taken to be exactly at 0 °C, and the water from melted ice mixes in at once.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Coastal cities like Mumbai and Chennai have mild days and nights compared with Delhi or Nagpur: the sea's huge mass of water, with its large specific heat, warms and cools slowly.
  • Car and bike engines are cooled with water, and hot-water bottles use water, because it carries a lot of heat per kilogram.
  • A few ice cubes cool a glass of nimbu-pani far more than the same mass of cold water would, because melting takes so much heat.
  • Food labs measure the energy in food by burning it in a bomb calorimeter and measuring the rise in water temperature.

📖 Key terms

  • Specific heat capacity c: heat needed to raise the temperature of unit mass by 1 °C (J/g°C or J/kg K).
  • Heat capacity: m × c, the heat a whole body needs per 1 °C (the copper can holds 38.5 J/°C).
  • Calorimeter: an insulated container, usually of copper, used to measure heat.
  • Latent heat of fusion: heat needed to melt unit mass of a solid without changing its temperature.
  • Thermal equilibrium: the state in which bodies in contact have the same temperature and no net heat flows.
