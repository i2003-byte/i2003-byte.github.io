📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

The heat of the Sun reaches us across empty space, where there is nothing to conduct or carry it. It travels by radiation. Every hot object — a heater, a fire, a hot iron — radiates heat in straight lines, and radiation needs no medium. When radiation falls on an object, some is reflected and some is absorbed. Dark, dull surfaces absorb more heat; light and shiny surfaces reflect most of it. Good absorbers are also good emitters, so a black can also cools faster.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

Radiation: heat travels without any medium, even through empty space Dark colours absorb more heat; light / shiny colours reflect more Closer to the heat source → more heat received

🌍 Real-world examples

  • We wear light-coloured clothes in summer and dark ones in winter
  • Solar cookers and water heaters are painted black inside
  • Shiny foil around food keeps it warm
  • We feel the warmth of a fire even though the air between isn’t hot

🎯 Try this

  1. Compare a black and a shiny can at 40 cm. Which gets hotter?
    Show answerThe black can — it absorbs about 95% of the radiation, the shiny one only about 10%.
  2. Move the cans to 80 cm. What happens to the heating?
    Show answerBoth heat up much more slowly — they receive far less radiation when farther away.
  3. Run the cooling race with a black and a shiny can. Which cools faster?
    Show answerThe black can — good absorbers are also good emitters of heat.
  4. Why should you wear white clothes on a hot summer day?
    Show answerWhite reflects most of the Sun’s heat, so you stay cooler.

📖 Key terms

  • Radiation — Transfer of heat without any medium.
  • Absorb — To take in heat.
  • Reflect — To bounce heat or light back.
