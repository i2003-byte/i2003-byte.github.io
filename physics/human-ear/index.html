📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

The outer ear collects sound and sends it down the ear canal to the eardrum — a thin membrane stretched tight like the skin of a drum. Sound makes the eardrum vibrate. Three tiny bones pass these vibrations to the inner ear (cochlea), which changes them into signals that travel along a nerve to the brain. That is how we hear!

🧮 Key formulas & rules

Sound → eardrum vibrates → tiny bones → cochlea → nerve → brain

the pathway of hearing

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Your ears “pop” in an aeroplane when the pressure on the eardrum changes
  • Hearing aids make sounds louder for people with weak hearing
  • Doctors warn never to put sharp objects into the ear — they can tear the eardrum

📖 Key terms

  • Eardrum — A thin stretched membrane that vibrates when sound reaches it.
  • Cochlea — The snail-shaped inner ear that turns vibrations into nerve signals.
  • Auditory nerve — The nerve that carries sound signals to the brain.
