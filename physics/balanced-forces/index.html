📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

A force is a push or a pull. When two forces act on the same object along one line, they combine into one net force. Pulls in the same direction add up; pulls in opposite directions subtract. If the two pulls are equal, the net force is zero and the forces are balanced: nothing about the motion changes. If one side pulls harder, the forces are unbalanced and the trolley starts moving, speeds up or slows down in the direction of the net force.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

net force = right pull − left pull

in newtons (N) · a positive answer means the net force points to the right

  • Net force = 0 → balanced: an object at rest stays at rest, a moving object keeps the same speed.
  • Net force ≠ 0 → unbalanced: the speed or the direction of motion changes.
acceleration a = F ÷ m

(Class 9) a bigger net force or a lighter trolley gives a bigger change of speed every second

Simplification: the floor and wheels are treated as perfectly smooth (no friction), so a moving trolley with balanced forces never slows down. On a real floor, friction would slowly stop it. You will meet friction in the next simulations.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • In a tug of war on sports day, the rope doesn't move while both teams pull equally hard. It moves towards the stronger team.
  • A book resting on a table: its weight pulls it down and the table pushes it up equally, so it stays still.
  • Two people pushing a stalled car from the back add their pushes, so the car moves more easily.

🎯 Try this

  1. Set the left pull to 200 N and the right pull to 250 N. What is the net force, and which way does the trolley go?
    Show answer250 − 200 = 50 N to the right. The trolley moves to the right, getting faster and faster.
  2. With a 50 kg trolley and a 50 N net force, what is the acceleration? How fast is it going after 3 s?
    Show answera = 50 ÷ 50 = 1 m/s². After 3 s its speed is 1 × 3 = 3 m/s. Check the velocity–time graph.
  3. While it is moving, press "Balance the forces". Does the trolley stop?
    Show answerNo. Balanced forces don't change the motion, so it keeps moving at the same speed. The velocity–time graph becomes a flat line.
  4. Now make the left pull bigger than the right. What happens to the speed?
    Show answerThe net force now points against the motion, so the trolley slows down, stops for a moment and then moves to the left.

📖 Key terms

  • Net force: the single force that has the same effect as all the forces acting together.
  • Balanced forces: forces whose net force is zero.
  • Newton (N): the SI unit of force. Lifting a 100 g packet of biscuits needs about 1 N.
