💡 The big idea

An oscillation is one complete to-and-fro motion: from one extreme position (A) to the other (B) and back to A. Timing just one swing is hard, so scientists time many oscillations and divide — this gives a much more accurate answer. From the count you can find the time period and the frequency.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

T = total time ÷ number of oscillations

time period, in seconds

f = number of oscillations ÷ total time = 1 ÷ T

frequency, in hertz

T = 2π √(L / g)

for a pendulum — depends on its length

🌍 Real-world examples

  • A swing in the park
  • The pendulum of a wall clock
  • A heartbeat measured by counting beats in one minute

🎯 Try this

  1. Count 10 oscillations of a 1 m pendulum. What is T? What is f?
    Show answerT ≈ 2.0 s, f ≈ 0.5 Hz.
  2. Switch to “I will count” and tap for each oscillation. How close is your answer?
    Show answerUsually within a few percent — counting more oscillations reduces the error.
  3. Make the string 4 times shorter (0.25 m). What happens to T?
    Show answerIt halves (≈ 1.0 s).
  4. Does pulling the bob further aside (a bigger angle) change T much?
    Show answerHardly at all for small angles.

📖 Key terms

  • Oscillation — One complete to-and-fro motion.
  • Time period — Time for one oscillation.
  • Frequency — Oscillations per second (Hz).
  • Mean position — The resting position (O) of the pendulum.
