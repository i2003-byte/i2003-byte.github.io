📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

When one end of a metal rod is heated, its particles vibrate faster and pass the heat on to their neighbours, one after another, all along the rod. This is conduction. The particles themselves stay in place — only the heat moves. Heat always flows from the hotter part to the colder part. Materials that let heat pass easily (metals such as copper and aluminium) are good conductors; those that don’t (wood, plastic, glass, air) are poor conductors or insulators.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

Heat flows from a hotter object to a colder one Conduction: heat passes from particle to particle in a solid Good conductors: copper, aluminium, iron (metals) Poor conductors (insulators): wood, plastic, glass, air

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Cooking pans are made of metal but have plastic or wooden handles
  • A metal spoon in hot tea gets hot at the other end
  • Woollen clothes trap air, a poor conductor, to keep us warm
  • Ice boxes use thermocol (an insulator) to keep things cold

🎯 Try this

  1. Which rod drops all its pins first: copper or iron?
    Show answerCopper — it is a better conductor than iron.
  2. Try glass or wood. Do any pins drop?
    Show answerNo — heat hardly moves along them. They are poor conductors (insulators).
  3. Why does the pin nearest the candle always drop first?
    Show answerHeat travels along the rod from the hot end, so the nearest wax melts first.
  4. Turn the candle off halfway through. What happens to the rod?
    Show answerIt cools down as it loses heat to the air — heat flows from the hot rod to the colder air.

📖 Key terms

  • Conduction — Transfer of heat from the hotter part of a solid to the colder part, particle to particle.
  • Conductor — A material that lets heat pass through it easily.
  • Insulator — A material that does not let heat pass easily.
