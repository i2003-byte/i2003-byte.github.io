📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Throw a ball up and it comes back. Throw it faster and it goes higher before it comes back. Gravity gets weaker as you go farther from a planet, so there is a special speed at which the ball keeps slowing down but never quite stops and never returns. This is the escape speed (often called escape velocity).

The energy bars show why. Kinetic energy (KE) is positive. Gravitational potential energy (PE) is negative near a planet and rises towards zero far away. If the total is negative, the probe is trapped and must fall back. If the total is zero or more, it escapes.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

PE per kg: −GM ÷ r KE per kg: ½ v² Escape speed: v_e = √(2GM ÷ R) = √(2 g R) Highest point (v < v_e): h = R v² ÷ (2gR − v²)

For Earth, g = 9.8 m/s² and R = 6371 km, so v_e = √(2 × 9.8 × 6.371 × 10⁶) ≈ 11.2 km/s. The escape speed does not depend on the mass of the object or on the direction of launch (if nothing gets in the way). For small speeds the formula for h becomes the familiar v² ÷ 2g.

Simplifications: no air resistance, the planet does not spin, and the probe gets all its speed at the start (like a cannon shell, not a rocket). The probe moves in a straight line from the planet's centre. Time is sped up so every flight takes a few seconds.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Real rockets, like ISRO's PSLV and LVM3, do not need 11.2 km/s at launch. Their engines keep pushing for many minutes, and the energy they give is what counts.
  • The Moon's escape speed is only 2.4 km/s, so it could not hold on to an atmosphere: gas molecules moving fast enough slowly leaked away into space.
  • Chandrayaan-3 did not get escape speed in one go. Its engine raised it step by step into longer and longer ellipses around the Earth until it could travel to the Moon and be captured by the Moon's gravity.
  • A black hole is so compact that its escape speed is more than the speed of light, so not even light can get out.

    Show answerNo. It reaches about 4.3 Earth radii (≈ 27 000 km) and falls back. Total energy is still negative.

    Show answerThe total stays at zero. The speed keeps falling but never reaches zero, so the probe never comes back.
  4. Switch to the Moon and launch at 2.0 km/s, then Jupiter at 2.0 km/s. Explain the difference.
    Show answerOn the Moon (v_e = 2.38 km/s) the probe climbs about 4 200 km. On Jupiter (v_e = 60.2 km/s) it rises only about 80 km, because Jupiter's gravity is much stronger.
  5. Does a heavier probe need a bigger escape speed?
    Show answerNo. Both its kinetic energy and its potential energy are proportional to its mass, so the mass cancels. A heavier probe needs more energy, but the same speed.

📖 Key terms

  • Escape speed: the smallest launch speed that lets an object get away from a planet's gravity for ever (ignoring air).
  • Gravitational potential energy: energy due to position in a gravitational field; taken as zero very far away, so it is negative near a planet.
  • Bound / unbound: total energy below zero (trapped) or at or above zero (free).
