📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Loudness is measured in decibels (dB). Normal talking is about 60 dB; busy traffic about 85 dB. The scale is special: every +10 dB sounds roughly twice as loud. Sounds above about 80 dB can harm hearing if you hear them for a long time, and sounds above about 120 dB are painful. Moving away from a source makes it quieter — about 6 dB less each time you double the distance.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

about −6 dB each time distance doubles

Above 80 dB → harmful over time

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Firecrackers and pressure horns are among the loudest city sounds
  • Workers near loud machines wear ear protection
  • Hospitals and schools are marked as “silence zones”

📖 Key terms

  • Decibel (dB) — The unit used to measure loudness.
  • Sound meter — An instrument that measures sound level in dB.
