📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Every object in the universe pulls every other object towards itself. This pull is called gravitation. Newton's universal law of gravitation says the force grows with the product of the two masses and falls with the square of the distance between their centres. The same law that makes an apple fall also keeps the Moon going round the Earth and the Earth going round the Sun. Between everyday objects the pull is far too tiny to notice; it only becomes large when at least one body is as heavy as a planet or a star.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

F = G m₁ m₂ ÷ r² G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻² (the universal gravitational constant) two students: 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 50 × 60 ÷ 1² ≈ 2.0 × 10⁻⁷ N
  • Double one mass: F doubles. Double both masses: F becomes 4 times.
  • Double the distance: F becomes ¼. Three times the distance: F becomes ⅑. This is the inverse-square law.
  • The two bodies pull each other with equal and opposite forces (Newton's third law). The lighter body gets the bigger acceleration, a = F ÷ m, so the apple falls towards the Earth, while the Earth's own movement towards the apple is far too small to measure.
  • r is measured between the centres of the bodies. For an apple on the ground, r is the Earth's radius, 6371 km.

Simplification: the bodies are treated as spheres (or points), with their mass acting at the centre. The drawing is not to scale; the sizes only hint at which body is heavier. Masses and distances are rounded real values.

🌍 In real life

  • The Earth's pull on you is your weight. Your pull on the Earth is exactly as big, but the Earth is so heavy that it does not move noticeably.
  • The Moon's pull on the oceans causes tides; at places like the Gulf of Khambhat the sea level rises and falls by several metres twice a day.
  • The Sun's gravity keeps all the planets in their orbits; the Earth's gravity keeps the Moon and satellites such as those launched by ISRO in their orbits.
  • Two students sitting next to each other do attract each other, but with less force than the weight of a speck of dust.

📖 Key terms

  • Gravitation: the force of attraction between any two objects that have mass.
  • Gravity: the gravitational pull of the Earth (or another planet) on objects near it.
  • Universal gravitational constant G: the force between two 1 kg masses 1 m apart, 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N; it is the same everywhere in the universe.
  • Inverse-square law: a quantity that falls as 1 ÷ distance².
