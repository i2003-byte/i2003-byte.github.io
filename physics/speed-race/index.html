📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Speed tells us how much distance an object covers in one unit of time. A racer at 5 m/s covers 5 metres every second. If two racers cover the same track, the one with the higher speed takes less time. Here each racer keeps a steady speed the whole way, so its motion is uniform and its line on the distance–time graph is straight.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

speed = distance ÷ time

so time = distance ÷ speed, and distance = speed × time

1 km/h = 1000 m ÷ 3600 s = 5/18 m/s

multiply km/h by 5/18 to get m/s (the SI unit); multiply m/s by 18/5 to go back

36 km/h = 36 × 5/18 = 10 m/s

a handy value to remember

Simplification: real racers speed up from rest at the start. Here they start at full speed, so the numbers match the formula exactly. Long, slow races are fast-forwarded on screen (look for ⏩), but the stopwatch shows the real race time.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • A speedometer in a bus or scooter shows speed in km/h; an odometer shows distance in km.
  • Indian Railways timetables let you work out a train’s average speed: distance between stations ÷ travel time.
  • A 100 m sprinter at about 10 m/s runs at roughly 36 km/h.
  • Traffic police use speed limits such as 50 km/h in cities.

🎯 Try this

  1. Set racer A to 36 km/h and the track to 100 m. Predict the time before you press Start.
    Show answer36 km/h = 10 m/s, so time = 100 ÷ 10 = 10 s.
  2. Racer B is twice as fast as racer A. How do their finishing times compare?
    Show answerB takes half the time of A, because time = distance ÷ speed.
  3. Make the track twice as long without changing speeds. What happens to each finishing time?
    Show answerEach time doubles. The winner’s lead in seconds also doubles.
  4. Look at the graph. Which line is steeper, and why?
    Show answerThe faster racer’s line is steeper: it gains more metres every second. The slope of a distance–time graph is the speed.
  5. A cyclist rides at 18 km/h. How far do they go in 1 minute?
    Show answer18 km/h = 5 m/s, and 5 m/s × 60 s = 300 m.

📖 Key terms

  • Speed: distance covered per unit time.
  • Uniform motion: equal distances in equal intervals of time, i.e. steady speed along a straight line.
  • m/s: metres per second, the SI unit of speed.
