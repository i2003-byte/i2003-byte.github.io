💡 The big idea

Once a ball leaves the cannon, the only force on it (ignoring air) is gravity, pulling straight down. So the motion splits into two independent parts: horizontally the ball keeps a constant speed, while vertically it slows down, stops at the top and speeds up on the way down. Together they trace a parabola.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

x = v₀ cos θ · t

horizontal position — constant speed

y = h + v₀ sin θ · t − ½ g t²

vertical position — constant acceleration

H = h + (v₀ sin θ)² / 2g

maximum height

R = v₀² sin 2θ / g (when h = 0)

range on level ground — biggest at θ = 45°

🌍 Real-world examples

  • A footballer taking a long kick or a cricketer hitting a six
  • Water from a garden hose or a fountain
  • A long-jumper leaving the board
  • Fireworks and the path of an arrow

🎯 Try this

  1. With height 0, find the angle that gives the longest range. Is it the same on the Moon?
    Show answer45° in both cases — gravity changes how far, but not the best angle.
  2. Launch at 30° and then at 60° with the same speed. What do you notice about the range?
    Show answerThe ranges are equal: complementary angles (adding to 90°) give the same range when h = 0.
  3. Raise the launch height to 20 m. Is 45° still the best angle?
    Show answerNo — from a height, a slightly lower angle (≈ 40° or less) goes further.
  4. Double the speed. How does the range change?
    Show answerIt becomes four times larger, because range ∝ v₀².
