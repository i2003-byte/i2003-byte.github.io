📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Electric power is how fast an appliance uses electrical energy, measured in watts (W). A 2000 W geyser uses energy about 200 times faster than a 9 W LED bulb. The energy used depends on both the power and how long the appliance runs.

Your electricity company does not count joules, which are far too small. It counts units: 1 unit is 1 kilowatt-hour (kWh), the energy a 1000 W appliance uses in one hour. The meter outside your home adds up the units, and the bill is units × rate.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

P = V I = I² R = V² ÷ R

1 W = 1 V × 1 A. A 1000 W iron on 220 V draws I = 1000 ÷ 220 ≈ 4.5 A and has R = 220² ÷ 1000 ≈ 48 Ω

Energy E = P × t, 1 kWh = 1000 W × 3600 s = 3.6 × 10⁶ J Heat produced H = I² R t (Joule's law of heating)
  • Units per day = (watts × hours) ÷ 1000, added up for every appliance.
  • Bill = units × rate in ₹ per unit.
  • Big-wattage appliances (AC, geyser, iron) cost the most even when used for a short time.

Simplification: one flat rate per unit. Real tariffs in India use slabs (each block of units costs more than the one before) and add fixed charges and taxes. Wattages are typical values; the fridge figure is its average power over the hours its compressor runs.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • A 9 W LED gives about the same light as a 60 W filament bulb. The rest of the filament bulb's energy becomes heat.
  • A star rating label (BEE) on an AC or fridge tells you how efficient it is; more stars means fewer units for the same job.
  • The heating effect (H = I²Rt) is useful in irons, geysers and toasters, but is wasted energy in fans, motors and wires.

📖 Key terms

  • Power: energy used per second, in watts (W).
  • Kilowatt-hour (unit): the energy used by 1 kW in 1 hour, 3.6 MJ.
  • Power rating: the power an appliance uses at its rated voltage (220 V in India).
  • Tariff: the price charged per unit of electricity.
