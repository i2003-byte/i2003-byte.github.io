📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Sunlight looks white, but it is a mixture of seven colours: violet, indigo, blue, green, yellow, orange and red (VIBGYOR). A prism splits white light into these colours. Newton's disc does the opposite: spin the painted colours fast and your eye mixes them back into a whitish colour.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

turns per second = rpm ÷ 60

time for one turn = 1 ÷ (turns per second)

  • The eye keeps each picture for about 1/16 of a second (persistence of vision). If each colour comes round faster than about 16 times a second, the colours overlap and blend.
  • The mixed colour is the average of the colours, weighted by how much of the disc each one covers.

Simplification: blending starts at 4 turns a second and is complete at 16. Paints are never pure colours, so a real disc looks off-white or light grey, not brilliant white. The rainbow sectors have different sizes (Newton used unequal ones too), chosen to make the mixture close to grey.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • A rainbow after the monsoon rain: raindrops split sunlight into its colours, like tiny prisms.
  • Films and cartoons: 24 or more still pictures a second blend into smooth motion for the same reason.
  • The blades of a fast ceiling fan blur into a single disc.

🎯 Try this

  1. Set the speed to 120 rpm, then 600 rpm, then 1500 rpm. When do the colours blend?
    Show answerAt 120 rpm (2 turns a second) you see each colour. At 600 rpm (10 a second) they flicker and start to merge. At 1500 rpm (25 a second) they blend into one colour.
  2. How long does one turn take at 1500 rpm?
    Show answer1500 ÷ 60 = 25 turns per second, so one turn takes 1 ÷ 25 s = 40 ms, much less than 1/16 s (about 62 ms).
  3. Spin the blue + yellow disc. What colour do you see? Is it green?
    Show answerNo, it turns grey. Mixing lights (or fast-changing colours) is different from mixing paints. Blue and yellow light together contain red, green and blue, so the result is a grey-white.
  4. Spin the red + green disc. What colour appears?
    Show answerA dull yellow. Red light and green light together look yellow to our eyes.

📖 Key terms

  • Persistence of vision: the eye holds an image for a short time after it has gone.
  • Dispersion: splitting white light into its colours, for example by a prism.
  • Spectrum: the band of colours (VIBGYOR) that makes up white light.
