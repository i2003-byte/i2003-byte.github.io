📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

A simple pendulum is a small heavy bob hanging from a light string. Pull it aside and let go: it swings to and fro in periodic motion, repeating itself in equal intervals of time. One oscillation is the trip from one extreme (A) to the other (B) and back to A. The time for one oscillation is the time period (T).

One oscillation is too quick to time well, so we time many (say 10 or 20) and divide. Your table shows the key result: the time period depends on the length of the string, not on the mass of the bob.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

T = total time ÷ number of oscillations

for example 14.2 s ÷ 10 = 1.42 s

one swing (A → B) = T ÷ 2

a pendulum clock ticks once per swing

T = 2π √(L ÷ g)

for older students (Class 11): L = length in m, g = 9.8 m/s². Four times the length gives twice the period.

Simplification: no air drag, so the swing never dies away, and the stopwatch starts exactly at release. In a real experiment your reaction time adds an error, which is one more reason to time many oscillations. Keep the swing small: large swings are slightly slower.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Old wall clocks in Indian homes and railway stations kept time with a swinging pendulum; turning a nut under the bob to change its length speeds the clock up or slows it down.
  • A seconds pendulum (about 99 cm long) swings once every second, so its time period is 2 s.
  • A child on a garden swing is a big pendulum: a longer swing moves back and forth more slowly.

🎯 Try this

  1. Keep L = 50 cm. Time 10 oscillations with the small bob, then with the big bob. What do you notice?
    Show answerThe time period is the same (about 1.42 s). The mass of the bob does not change the period.
  2. Now try 25 cm, 50 cm and 1 m. How does T change as the string gets longer?
    Show answerA longer string gives a longer time period: about 1.0 s, 1.42 s and 2.0 s. Doubling the length does not double T, though; you need four times the length for that.
  3. Pick the “Seconds pendulum” preset. How long is one swing (one tick)?
    Show answerAbout 1 second. T ≈ 2 s, and one swing is half an oscillation.
  4. A pendulum makes 20 oscillations in 32 s. What is its time period?
    Show answerT = 32 ÷ 20 = 1.6 s.
  5. A pendulum clock runs slow (it loses time). Should you make its pendulum longer or shorter?
    Show answerShorter. A shorter pendulum has a smaller time period, so it ticks faster.

📖 Key terms

  • Oscillation: one complete to-and-fro movement, A → B → A.
  • Time period: time taken for one oscillation, in seconds.
  • Mean position (O): where the bob hangs at rest.
  • Periodic motion: motion that repeats itself after equal intervals of time.
