📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Sound travels by making the particles of a medium vibrate and pass the vibration to their neighbours. In solids the particles are packed tightly and strongly connected, so the vibration is passed on fastest. In liquids it is slower, and in gases like air it is slowest. Warmer air carries sound a little faster.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

speed = distance ÷ time v(steel) ≈ 5960 m/s > v(water) ≈ 1480 m/s > v(air) ≈ 343 m/s

at room temperature

v(air) ≈ 331 + 0.6 × T m/s

T in °C

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Putting your ear to a railway track to hear a distant train
  • Whales communicating over huge distances under water
  • A string-and-cup telephone
  • Hearing knocks through a wall or table

  1. For a 1000 m track, how many times faster is steel than air?
    Show answerAbout 17 times faster (≈ 0.17 s vs 2.9 s).
  2. Raise the air temperature from 0 °C to 40 °C. What happens?
    Show answerSound in air gets faster — from about 331 m/s to 355 m/s.
  3. Why can you hear a train through the rails before you hear it through the air?
    Show answerSound travels much faster in steel (a solid) than in air.
  4. Which is faster: wood or water?
    Show answerWood — it is a solid.

📖 Key terms

  • Medium — The material (solid, liquid or gas) through which sound travels.
  • Speed of sound — The distance sound travels in one second.
