📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Resistors can be joined in two basic ways. In series they sit one after another, so the same charges pass through every one of them: the current is the same everywhere and the battery's voltage is shared between them. In parallel each resistor has its own path between the same two points, so each gets the full voltage and the battery's current splits between the paths.

Adding resistors in series makes the total resistance bigger. Adding them in parallel gives the current more paths, so the total resistance becomes smaller than the smallest one.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

Series: R = R₁ + R₂ + R₃

same current I = V ÷ R through each; V = V₁ + V₂ + V₃, with Vₖ = I Rₖ

Parallel: 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃

same voltage V across each; I = I₁ + I₂ + I₃, with Iₖ = V ÷ Rₖ

  • In series, the biggest resistor takes the biggest share of the voltage.
  • In parallel, the smallest resistor carries the biggest current.
  • For two resistors in parallel, R = R₁R₂ ÷ (R₁ + R₂). Two equal resistors in parallel give half of one.

Simplification: the battery, wires and meters are ideal (no resistance of their own), so the full battery voltage reaches the resistors.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Everything in your home is wired in parallel: each appliance gets the full 220 V and can be switched on and off on its own.
  • Cheap decorative “serial” lights are in series: when one bulb fails, the whole string goes dark.
  • Switching on more appliances adds more parallel paths, so the total resistance falls and the current from the mains rises. That is why overloading blows a fuse.

🎯 Try this

  1. In series with 6 V and R₁ = 5, R₂ = 10, R₃ = 15 Ω, find R, I and the voltage across each.
    Show answerR = 30 Ω, I = 6 ÷ 30 = 0.2 A. V₁ = 1 V, V₂ = 2 V, V₃ = 3 V, which add up to 6 V.
  2. Switch to parallel with the same resistors. What is the total current, and which resistor carries the most?
    Show answer1/R = 1/5 + 1/10 + 1/15, so R ≈ 2.73 Ω and I = 2.2 A. R₁ (5 Ω) carries the most: 1.2 A, then 0.6 A and 0.4 A.
  3. Turn on “Break R₂” in series and then in parallel. What is different?
    Show answerIn series all the current stops, and the full 6 V appears across the gap. In parallel only R₂'s branch stops; the other two carry the same current as before.
  4. In parallel, untick R₃ and set R₁ = R₂ = 10 Ω. What is R?
    Show answer5 Ω: two equal resistors in parallel give half of one.
  5. Is the parallel total ever larger than the smallest resistor? Try a few values.
    Show answerNever. Each new path lets more current flow, so R is always less than the smallest single resistance.

📖 Key terms

  • Series: components joined end to end in a single path.
  • Parallel: components joined between the same two points, each in its own branch.
  • Equivalent resistance: the single resistance that would draw the same current from the battery.
  • Junction: a point where the current splits or joins; the current in equals the current out.
