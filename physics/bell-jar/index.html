📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Sound needs a medium — particles that can vibrate — to travel. When air is pumped out of the jar there are fewer and fewer particles to carry the vibrations, so the sound gets fainter. In a complete vacuum (empty space) sound cannot travel at all, even though you can still see the hammer hitting the bell, because light does not need a medium.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

Less air → fainter sound Vacuum → no sound at all

sound cannot travel through empty space

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Astronauts on the Moon use radios to talk — there is no air to carry their voices
  • Explosions in space are silent (films add the sound!)
  • Double-glazed windows with a thin vacuum or gas gap reduce outside noise

🎯 Try this

  1. Pump out all the air. Can you still see the bell ringing? Can you hear it?
    Show answerYou can see it but not hear it — light travels through a vacuum, sound does not.
  2. Let the air back in halfway. What happens to the loudness?
    Show answerThe sound returns, at about half the loudness.
  3. Why can’t astronauts talk to each other directly on the Moon?
    Show answerThere is no air on the Moon to carry sound.

📖 Key terms

  • Vacuum — A space with no air or other particles.
  • Medium — The material through which sound travels.
