📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

The frequency is the number of oscillations (vibrations) per second, measured in hertz (Hz). The time period is the time for one oscillation. A high frequency means a high pitch (shrill sound, like a whistle); a low frequency gives a low pitch (a deep sound, like a lion’s roar or a drum).

🧮 Key formulas & rules

frequency f = number of oscillations ÷ time

unit: hertz (Hz) = 1 per second

time period T = 1 ÷ f

e.g. 500 Hz → T = 0.002 s = 2 ms

Higher frequency → higher pitch

🌍 Real-world examples

  • A woman’s or child’s voice usually has a higher pitch than a man’s
  • A bird’s chirp is high-pitched; a lion’s roar is low-pitched
  • Thin strings on a guitar play higher notes than thick ones

📖 Key terms

  • Frequency — Number of oscillations per second (Hz).
  • Time period — Time taken for one complete oscillation.
  • Pitch — How high or low a sound seems; depends on frequency.
  • Hertz (Hz) — Unit of frequency: one oscillation per second.
