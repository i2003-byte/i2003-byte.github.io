📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

An electric circuit is a complete path for electric current. Current flows from the positive (+) terminal of the battery, through the wires, switch and bulb, and back to the negative (−) terminal. If there is a break anywhere (a switch turned OFF, a loose wire or a broken filament) the circuit is open and no current flows.

Drawing real objects takes time, so we use circuit symbols. Switch the view to see the same circuit as a circuit diagram.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

battery = cells joined + to −

each dry cell gives about 1.5 V, so 2 cells give 3 V

  • Cell symbol: a long thin line (+) and a short thick line (−).
  • Switch: a gap that the lever closes (ON) or opens (OFF).
  • Bulb: a circle with the filament inside; a fused bulb has a broken filament.

Simplification: each bulb is treated as a fixed 10 Ω resistor and cells have no internal resistance. The current values are only a model; in Class 7 focus on “open or closed” and “brighter or dimmer”. The moving dots show the conventional direction of current, from + to −.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • A torch: two cells placed one after the other, a switch on the side and a bulb at the front.
  • Fairy lights for Diwali: when one old-style bulb in a series string fuses, the whole string goes dark.
  • Every switch board at home opens or closes a circuit to turn a light or fan on and off.

🎯 Try this

  1. Tap the switch. Why does the bulb light only when the switch is ON?
    Show answerON closes the gap, so the path round the loop is complete and current can flow.
  2. Turn on “Fuse a bulb” with the switch ON. What happens, and why?
    Show answerThe bulb goes out and the current stops. The broken filament leaves a gap in the circuit, just like an open switch.
  3. With 2 cells, turn one cell the wrong way. What happens?
    Show answerThe two cells push in opposite directions and cancel, so no current flows. Cells must be joined + to −.
  4. Add a second bulb in series. Are the bulbs brighter or dimmer? How can you make them normal again?
    Show answerDimmer, because the same battery now pushes current through two bulbs. Using 4 cells brings them back to normal brightness.
  5. Draw a circuit with 3 cells, a switch and one bulb using symbols. Then check it with the diagram view.
    Show answerThree cell symbols in a row (long line of one next to the short line of the next), a switch symbol and a bulb symbol, all joined in one loop.

📖 Key terms

  • Closed circuit: a complete path, so current flows.
  • Open circuit: a break in the path, so no current flows.
  • Battery: two or more cells joined together.
  • Filament: the thin coiled wire inside a bulb that glows.
