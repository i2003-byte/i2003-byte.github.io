📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

We use words like hot and cold, but how hot something is can only be measured reliably with a thermometer. The number it gives is the temperature, measured in degrees Celsius (°C). In a hotter object the tiny particles move faster. Our hand only tells us whether something is hotter or colder than our hand — so the same lukewarm water can feel warm to one hand and cold to the other.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

Temperature is measured in degrees Celsius (°C) Water freezes at 0 °C and boils at 100 °C Normal body temperature ≈ 37 °C °F = °C × 9/5 + 32

🌍 Real-world examples

  • A cup of tea feels hot; an ice cube feels cold
  • Floor tiles feel colder than a carpet at the same temperature
  • Weather reports give the air temperature in °C
  • Doctors measure body temperature to check for fever

🎯 Try this

  1. Set the hand to “Ice-cold water” and the water to 30 °C. How does it feel?
    Show answerHot! The water is much warmer than your chilled hand.
  2. Now set the hand to “Hot water” with the same 30 °C water. How does it feel now?
    Show answerCold! The water did not change, only your hand did. Touch is not a reliable way to measure temperature.
  3. What happens to the particles as you go from 20 °C to 90 °C?
    Show answerThey move faster — higher temperature means faster-moving particles.
  4. What does 37 °C become in °F?
    Show answer37 × 9/5 + 32 = 98.6 °F.

📖 Key terms

  • Temperature — A measure of how hot or cold something is.
  • Degree Celsius (°C) — The unit of temperature we use most often.
  • Thermometer — An instrument that measures temperature.
