📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Every spring, swing, bridge or guitar string has its own natural frequency f₀: the rate at which it oscillates if you disturb it and leave it alone. Here a motor keeps shaking the top of the spring at a driving frequency that you choose. This gives forced oscillations.

After a short wobble at the start, the block settles down and oscillates at the motor's frequency, not its own. How big the swing becomes depends on how close the two frequencies are. When the driving frequency matches the natural frequency, each push arrives at just the right moment and the amplitude becomes very large. This is resonance. Damping (friction) decides how tall and sharp the peak is.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

Natural frequency: f₀ = (1 ÷ 2π) √(k ÷ m) Equation: m a = −k (x − a₀ sin ωt) − b v Steady amplitude: A = (k a₀ ÷ m) ÷ √((ω₀² − ω²)² + (bω ÷ m)²)

ω = 2πf is the angular driving frequency and ω₀ = 2πf₀. The motor moves the top of the spring by a₀ = 1 cm, which acts like a driving force of k·a₀. At resonance with light damping, A ≈ k a₀ ÷ (b ω₀), so halving the damping doubles the peak. Far below f₀ the block simply follows the motor (A ≈ a₀). Far above f₀ it hardly moves.

Simplifications: the mass is 1 kg, the spring has no mass and obeys Hooke's law, and damping is a drag force −bv. Gravity only shifts the rest position of a hanging spring, so x is measured from that rest position. A point is added to the curve when the measured amplitude stops changing from one cycle to the next.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Pushing a friend on a swing: small pushes at the swing's own rhythm make it go very high. Pushing at the wrong rhythm does little.
  • Tuning a radio or a TV: the circuit's natural frequency is changed until it matches one station's frequency, which it then picks up strongly.
  • Soldiers marching across a bridge are told to break step, so their regular footsteps do not drive the bridge at its natural frequency.
  • A tabla, sitar or guitar body resonates with the string, which makes the sound much louder.
  • Engineers add dampers to tall buildings and keep machine speeds away from the natural frequency of their mountings, so they do not shake themselves apart.

📖 Key terms

  • Free oscillation: oscillation at the natural frequency after a single disturbance.
  • Forced oscillation: oscillation at the frequency of an outside periodic force.
  • Resonance: the large amplitude reached when the driving frequency is close to the natural frequency.
  • Damping: loss of energy (to air, friction, heat) that limits the amplitude.
