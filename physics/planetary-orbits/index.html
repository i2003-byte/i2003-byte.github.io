📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

The Sun's gravity pulls every planet towards it. A planet does not fall in because it is also moving sideways very fast. The pull keeps bending its path, so it goes round and round. Newton showed that this one rule, gravity weakening as 1/r², explains the three laws that Johannes Kepler had found from careful observations of Mars:

  1. Law of orbits: every planet moves on an ellipse with the Sun at one focus (a circle is a special ellipse).
  2. Law of areas: the line from the Sun to the planet sweeps out equal areas in equal times. So the planet moves fastest when it is closest (perihelion) and slowest when farthest (aphelion).
  3. Law of periods: T² is proportional to a³, where a is the semi-major axis.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

Gravity: F = G M m ÷ r² Circular speed: v = √(GM ÷ r) Escape speed: v = √(2GM ÷ r) Kepler 3: T² = (4π² ÷ GM) a³ → T² = a³ (T in years, a in AU, Sun's mass) Ellipse: r_peri = a(1 − e), r_aph = a(1 + e), area = π a b

e is the eccentricity: 0 for a circle, close to 1 for a long, thin ellipse. Angular momentum is conserved, so r × v is the same at perihelion and aphelion. With a star of mass M (in solar masses), T² ÷ a³ = 1 ÷ M.

How the simulation works: the planet is pushed by Newton's law of gravity in small time steps. The dashed curve is the exact ellipse worked out from the starting distance and speed. Each finished orbit is cut into 12 equal-time sectors; compare "Last sector area" with πab ÷ 12. Simplifications: the star does not move, and the other planets' pulls are ignored.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Earth's orbit is nearly a circle (e = 0.017). We are closest to the Sun in early January. Seasons come from the tilt of Earth's axis, not from this distance.
  • Halley's comet has e ≈ 0.97. It races past the Sun every 75 to 76 years and spends most of its time far beyond Neptune. It will next be seen in 2061.
  • ISRO's Mars Orbiter Mission (Mangalyaan) travelled to Mars on a half-ellipse that touched Earth's orbit at one end and Mars's orbit at the other.

📖 Key terms

  • Focus (plural foci): one of two special points inside an ellipse. The Sun sits at one of them; the other is empty.
  • Semi-major axis (a): half the longest width of the ellipse; the average of the nearest and farthest distances.
  • Perihelion / aphelion: the nearest and farthest points of an orbit from the Sun.
  • AU (astronomical unit): the average Earth–Sun distance, about 1.5 × 10⁸ km.
