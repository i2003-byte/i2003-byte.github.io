📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Inside a storm cloud, strong air currents rub ice and water particles together, and charges separate: the bottom of the cloud usually becomes negative. This pulls a positive charge on to the ground below, crowded on tall, pointed things. When the charges become very large, the air can no longer keep them apart. A spark jumps between the cloud and the ground: that is lightning. It takes the shortest path, so it usually strikes the tallest object nearby. A lightning conductor is a set of metal rods on top of a building joined by a thick copper strip to a metal plate buried in the earth. The flash strikes the rods and the current flows safely into the ground instead of through the walls.

  • A flash carries about 5 to 20 coulombs of charge at around 100 million volts, with a current of about 30 000 amperes for a fraction of a second.
  • The flash heats the air to about 30 000 °C. The air expands suddenly and we hear this as thunder.

🌍 Lightning safety

  • Go inside a building or a car with a metal roof. Stay away from open fields, hilltops, water and tall trees.
  • Never shelter under a lone tree: lightning often strikes the tree and can jump sideways to a person standing under it (a side flash).
  • Indoors, keep away from taps, wired phones and plugged-in appliances; unplug the TV and computer if you can.
  • If you are caught in the open, keep away from tall things, crouch low with your feet together and head tucked in, and move to shelter as soon as you can.
  • The India Meteorological Department and the Damini app give lightning warnings. Lightning kills more people in India than any other natural hazard, so these warnings matter.

🎯 Try this

  1. Run 20 quick strikes a few times without the conductor, then turn the conductor on and clear the tally. What happens to the number of hits on the house?
    Show answerWithout it, the roof is hit in about 4 of every 10 flashes. With the conductor the roof is not hit at all: the flash lands on the rods and flows down the copper strip into the earth.
  2. Put the person under the tall tree and let several flashes happen. Why is this a dangerous place?
    Show answerThe tree is the tallest thing around, so it gets struck about a third of the time. Each time, the current can jump from the tree to the person (a side flash).
  3. Compare "standing in an open field" with "crouching low". Is crouching safe?
    Show answerCrouching makes the person a slightly smaller target (fewer hits over many flashes), but they are still struck sometimes. Nowhere outside is safe; the best plan is to get indoors.
  4. Set your distance to 3 km. How long after the flash do you hear the thunder?
    Show answerAbout 8.7 s (3000 m ÷ 343 m/s). Counting about 9 seconds and dividing by 3 gives 3 km.

📖 Key terms

  • Lightning: a giant electric spark between a cloud and the ground or between clouds.
  • Lightning conductor: metal rods on top of a building, joined to the earth by a thick metal strip, that carry lightning safely into the ground.
  • Earthing: connecting something to the ground so that charge can flow away safely.
  • Discharge: the sudden flow of charge that cancels a build-up of charge, such as a spark.
