📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Light travels more slowly in glass and water than in air. When a ray crosses a surface at an angle, this change of speed makes it change direction: this is refraction. Going from air into the slab (into a denser medium) the ray bends towards the normal; coming out into air it bends away from the normal. Because the two faces of a slab are parallel, the second bend exactly undoes the first: the ray comes out parallel to the incoming ray, just shifted sideways. For each material, sin i ÷ sin r is always the same number, the refractive index n. That is Snell's law.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

sin i ÷ sin r = n (Snell's law) n = c ÷ v e = i lateral shift d = t sin(i − r) ÷ cos r Glass n = 1.52, i = 40°: sin r = 0.643 ÷ 1.52 = 0.423 → r ≈ 25.0°
  • Angles are always measured from the normal, the line at 90° to the surface.
  • The incident ray, the refracted ray and the normal all lie in one plane.
  • A ray along the normal (i = 0°) goes straight through without bending.
  • Refractive indices: water 1.33, acrylic 1.49, crown glass 1.52, dense flint glass 1.65, diamond 2.42. A bigger n means slower light and more bending.
  • The lateral shift grows with the thickness of the slab and with the angle of incidence.

Simplifications: the light has one colour (real white light splits slightly into colours), and the weak reflected rays at each face are not drawn.

🌍 In real life

  • Letters seen through a thick glass block on a table look shifted and raised.
  • A pool or a bucket of water looks shallower than it really is, and a straight stick dipped in water looks bent.
  • Diamonds sparkle partly because their very high refractive index bends light strongly.
  • In the school lab, you trace this with pins on paper: incident pins, then emergent pins seen through the slab.

  1. Crown glass, i = 40°. Check r and the ratio sin i ÷ sin r.
    Show answerr ≈ 25.0°, and sin 40° ÷ sin 25.0° = 0.643 ÷ 0.423 ≈ 1.52, the refractive index of the glass.
  2. Record readings at 20°, 40°, 60° and 80°. What shape do the points make on the chart?
    Show answerA straight line through the origin. Its slope is n, which is Snell's law: sin i = n × sin r.
  3. Keep i = 60° and change the slab from water to diamond. What happens to r?
    Show answerWater: r ≈ 40.6°. Diamond: r ≈ 21.0°. The bigger the refractive index, the more the ray bends towards the normal.
  4. Double the thickness from 5 cm to 10 cm. What happens to the lateral shift?
    Show answerIt doubles too: d = t sin(i − r) ÷ cos r is proportional to t, while the angles do not change.
  5. How fast does light travel in crown glass?
    Show answerv = c ÷ n = 3 × 10⁸ ÷ 1.52 ≈ 1.97 × 10⁸ m/s, about two thirds of its speed in air.

📖 Key terms

  • Refraction: the bending of light as it passes from one medium into another.
  • Normal: the line at right angles to the surface at the point where the ray strikes.
  • Refractive index (n): the speed of light in air (vacuum) ÷ its speed in the medium.
  • Angle of emergence (e): the angle between the outgoing ray and the normal at the second face.
  • Lateral shift: the sideways distance between the incident ray (continued) and the emergent ray.
