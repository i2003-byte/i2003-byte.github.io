📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

A cart at the top of a roller coaster has potential energy because of its height. As it runs down, it loses height and gains speed: potential energy turns into kinetic energy. Going up the next hump, the change runs the other way. On an ideal track with no friction, the total PE + KE never changes, so the cart always rises again to exactly the height it started from, and never higher. This is the law of conservation of energy: energy is never made or destroyed, only changed from one form to another. With friction, a little energy turns into heat on every metre, so the cart cannot quite get back up, and it finally settles in a valley.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

PE = m g h KE = ½ m v² g = 9.8 m/s² PE + KE (+ heat) = m g h₀ = constant Speed after falling through a height h: v = √(2 g h)
  • 400 kg released from 30 m: total energy = 400 × 9.8 × 30 = 117 600 J = 117.6 kJ.
  • The speed at any height depends only on how far the cart has dropped, not on its mass and not on the shape of the track (when there is no friction).
  • To get over a hump the cart must start higher than the hump.

Simplifications: the cart is held on the rails (real coasters have wheels under the track too), there is no air drag, and friction is taken as μ g cos α, ignoring the extra push on curved parts of the track. Heights are drawn more stretched than lengths.

🌍 In real life

  • Rides at amusement parks such as those in big Indian cities start with a lift up the tallest hill: that one climb gives the cart all the energy for the whole ride, so every later hill must be lower.
  • A child on a swing, a pendulum, and a ball rolling in a bowl all keep trading PE and KE.
  • Hydroelectric dams like Bhakra Nangal let water fall: its PE becomes KE that spins turbines.
  • Brakes on a bicycle going downhill turn the energy into heat in the brake pads, just like the friction setting here.

🎯 Try this

  1. With no friction and h = 30 m, how fast is the cart at the lowest point (0 m)?
    Show answerv = √(2 × 9.8 × 30) = √588 ≈ 24.2 m/s, about 87 km/h. All 30 m of height has become speed.
  2. Change the mass from 400 kg to 1000 kg. What happens to the speed and to the energy?
    Show answerThe speed at every point is the same, because both PE and KE are proportional to m and m cancels. The energies (in kJ) become 2.5 times bigger.
  3. Release the cart from 15 m. Why can't it cross the hump?
    Show answerThe hump is 18 m high. To be there the cart would need PE = m g × 18, which is more than its total energy m g × 15. It rolls back and keeps swinging in the first valley.
  4. From 30 m with no friction, how fast is the cart on top of the 18 m hump?
    Show answerIt has dropped 30 − 18 = 12 m, so v = √(2 × 9.8 × 12) ≈ 15.3 m/s.
  5. Turn on friction. Watch the red "highest it can now reach" line. Why does it keep going down?
    Show answerFriction turns some energy into heat on every metre. The energy left for PE + KE gets smaller, so the greatest height the cart could reach also drops, until the cart can no longer climb out of a valley.

📖 Key terms

  • Potential energy (PE): energy stored because of position, here the height above the ground.
  • Kinetic energy (KE): energy of motion.
  • Mechanical energy: PE + KE.
  • Conservation of energy: the total energy stays the same; it only changes form.
