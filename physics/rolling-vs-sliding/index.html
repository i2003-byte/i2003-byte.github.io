📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

When one body rolls over another, the friction is much smaller than when it slides. A wheel doesn't scrape its bumps across the floor; it lifts off them as it turns. That is why a toy car on wheels runs several times farther than a wooden block let go from the same height. On every surface, the rougher the floor, the sooner friction stops the object.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

on the ramp: a = g (sin θ − μ cos θ) stopping distance d = v² ÷ (2 μ g)

v = speed at the foot of the ramp · μ = friction coefficient of the floor · g = 9.8 m/s²

  • Rolling friction < sliding friction < static friction.
  • For a fair test, change only one thing at a time: the object or the floor, always from the same height.

Simplifications: the ramp is 30° and made of wood, the object keeps its speed as it turns on to the floor, the car's wheels are treated as light and there is no air resistance. The μ values are typical classroom values, not measurements.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Suitcases have wheels, so pulling one through a railway station is easy.
  • Ball bearings in cycle hubs and ceiling fans replace sliding with rolling to cut friction.
  • Carrom players sprinkle powder on the board so the striker slides with less friction.
  • Sand on a road makes a cycle slow down quickly: a sandy road has high friction.

🎯 Try this

  1. From h = 20 cm on smooth tile, how far does the toy car run? And the block?
    Show answerThe car runs about 146 cm, the block only about 32 cm: more than four times less.
  2. Fill in the whole table from the same height. Which floor stops the car soonest?
    Show answerSand, where the car runs only about 39 cm from 20 cm. Sand has the largest friction.
  3. Halve the height to 10 cm. What happens to the distance on the floor?
    Show answerIt halves too. The speed squared at the foot of the ramp is proportional to the height, and d = v² ÷ (2 μ g).
  4. Why must the height stay the same while you compare surfaces?
    Show answerA different height gives a different starting speed, so you couldn't tell whether the surface or the speed caused the change. Keeping it the same makes it a fair test.

📖 Key terms

  • Rolling friction: friction when a body rolls over a surface.
  • Sliding friction: friction when one surface slides over another.
  • Fair test: an experiment where only one thing is changed at a time.
