📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Light travels in straight lines. In a dark box with a tiny hole, light from the top of a candle can only reach the bottom of the screen, and light from the bottom can only reach the top. So a pinhole camera makes an image that is upside down, and left and right are swapped too (the image is turned through 180°).

🧮 Key formulas & rules

image height = object height × v ÷ u

u: object to pinhole · v: pinhole to screen (the two triangles through the hole are similar)

  • A longer box (bigger v) or a nearer object (smaller u) gives a bigger image.
  • A bigger hole lets in more light, so the image is brighter, but each point becomes a patch, so it is blurrier. Patch size ≈ d × (u + v) ÷ u.

Simplification: brightness is shown relative to a 1 mm hole with u = 1 m and v = 20 cm. We ignore the spreading of light (diffraction) that makes extremely tiny holes blurry again.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • During a solar eclipse, gaps between leaves act as pinholes and throw many small images of the crescent Sun on the ground.
  • Our eye works like a camera: the pupil lets light in and an upside-down image forms on the retina. The brain turns it the right way up.
  • Old astronomers watched the Sun safely through a pinhole in a dark room (a "camera obscura", which means dark room).

🎯 Try this

  1. Switch to the letter F. How does it look on the screen?
    Show answerUpside down and reversed left to right: the F is turned through 180°. Every ray crosses over at the hole.
  2. With h = 20 cm, u = 100 cm and v = 20 cm, what is the image height? Check with the readout.
    Show answer20 × 20 ÷ 100 = 4 cm, five times smaller than the candle.
  3. Move the candle closer to the pinhole. What happens to the image?
    Show answerIt grows, because u gets smaller. At u = v it is the same size as the candle.
  4. Change the pinhole from 0.5 mm to 10 mm. What do you gain and what do you lose?
    Show answerYou gain brightness (more light gets in) but lose sharpness: each point of the candle lights a bigger patch, so the patches overlap and the image blurs.

📖 Key terms

  • Rectilinear propagation: light travels in straight lines.
  • Inverted image: an image that is upside down.
  • Screen: a surface (here tracing paper) on which an image can be caught.
