📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Your eye works like a camera: the cornea and the eye lens together form a real, inverted image on the retina, the light-sensitive screen at the back. But the distance from lens to retina cannot change (about 2.3 cm). So to see both far and near things sharply, the eye changes the focal length of its lens. The ciliary muscles do this. Relaxed, they let the lens become thin (weak, long focal length) for far objects; contracted, they let the lens bulge (strong, short focal length) for near objects. This ability is called the power of accommodation. Bring the object closer and watch the lens thicken until it can bulge no more: that is the near point.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

1/v − 1/u = 1/f with v = 2.3 cm fixed → P needed = 100/2.3 + 100/d ≈ 43.5 D + 100/d (d in cm) Object at 25 cm: P = 43.5 + 4 = 47.5 D Object very far: P = 43.5 D
  • Far point: the farthest point seen clearly; for a normal eye it is at infinity.
  • Near point (least distance of distinct vision): the closest point seen clearly; about 25 cm for a young adult.
  • Power of accommodation = most power − least power = 100/(near point in cm) dioptres, about 4 D for a near point of 25 cm.
  • The near point moves away with age as the lens stiffens: a child can focus at about 7–10 cm, but many people over 45 need reading glasses (presbyopia).

Simplification: this is a "reduced eye" with one lens doing all the focusing and a fixed 2.3 cm to the retina. In a real eye the cornea gives about two-thirds of the power and the lens changes the rest. The distances outside the eye are drawn on a squashed (logarithmic) scale, the spread of rays at the retina is drawn 3 times bigger so you can see it, and the ages are rough averages.

🌍 In real life

  • Read a book for a long time and your eyes feel tired: the ciliary muscles have been contracted all along. Looking far away (the "20-20-20" habit) lets them relax.
  • Grandparents often hold a newspaper at arm's length: their near point has moved far away.
  • A camera focuses by moving its lens; a fish's eye also moves its lens. Our eye changes the lens shape instead.
  • The pupil (the hole in the coloured iris) only controls how much light comes in; it does not focus.

  4. What is the focal length of the eye when it looks at a far object?

📖 Key terms

  • Retina: the light-sensitive layer of rods and cones; it sends signals through the optic nerve to the brain.
  • Ciliary muscles: the ring of muscles that changes the shape of the eye lens.
  • Accommodation: the eye's ability to change its focal length.
  • Presbyopia: loss of accommodation with age.
