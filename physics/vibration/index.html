📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

All sound is produced by vibrating objects. A vibration is a rapid to-and-fro (back-and-forth) movement. When a tuning fork is struck its prongs vibrate so fast that you cannot see them — but a ping-pong ball touching it gets kicked away! When you touch the object, the vibration stops, and so does the sound.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

No vibration → no sound

every sound has a vibrating source

Vibration = to-and-fro motion of an object

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Your vocal cords vibrate when you speak
  • The skin (membrane) of a drum or tabla
  • Strings of a sitar, veena or guitar
  • Air column inside a flute or whistle
  • Wings of a mosquito or bee (the buzz!)

🎯 Try this

  1. Strike the tuning fork, then press “Touch to stop”. What happens to the sound?
    Show answerIt stops at once — no vibration means no sound.
  2. Which object keeps ringing the longest? Which stops quickest?
    Show answerThe tuning fork rings longest; the drum dies away quickest.
  3. Turn on the ping-pong ball test. Why does the ball jump?
    Show answerThe invisible vibrations of the prong push it away — proof the fork is vibrating.
  4. Strike softly and then hard. What changes?
    Show answerA harder strike gives bigger vibrations (larger amplitude) and a louder sound.

📖 Key terms

  • Vibration — A rapid to-and-fro motion of an object.
  • Tuning fork — A two-pronged metal fork that vibrates at one fixed frequency when struck.
  • Membrane — A thin stretched sheet, like the skin of a drum.
