📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

In liquids and gases, heat mostly travels by convection. Water near the flame gets hot, becomes lighter and rises. Cooler, heavier water from the top sinks to take its place, gets heated, and rises in turn. This circulation is a convection current — the moving water itself carries the heat. A crystal of potassium permanganate leaves a purple trail that shows the current.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

Convection: heat is carried by the movement of the liquid or gas itself Hot water / air rises; cool water / air sinks Convection needs a liquid or gas — it cannot happen in a solid

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Water in a pan is heated from the bottom, yet the whole pan gets hot
  • Smoke and hot air rise from a fire
  • Ventilators are placed near the ceiling so hot air can escape
  • Sea and land breezes are giant convection currents in air

🎯 Try this

  1. Drop a crystal and watch the purple streak. Which way does it go first?
    Show answerUp the side above the burner, across the top, down the far side and back along the bottom.
  2. Move the burner to the right corner. What changes?
    Show answerThe current turns the other way — the water always rises above the heat.
  3. Turn the flame off. What happens to the current?
    Show answerIt slows down and stops, because there is no more hot water to rise.
  4. Why does the top of the water get hot before the bottom far from the flame?
    Show answerHot water rises, so the heat is carried upward to the top first.

📖 Key terms

  • Convection — Transfer of heat by the movement of a liquid or gas.
  • Convection current — The loop of rising hot fluid and sinking cool fluid.
