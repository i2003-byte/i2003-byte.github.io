📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

When an object goes round a circle at a steady speed, it is in uniform circular motion. Its speed does not change, but its direction changes at every moment: at each point it is heading along the tangent to the circle (the line that just touches the circle there). Velocity means speed and direction, so a change of direction is a change of velocity. That is why uniform circular motion is an accelerated motion, even though the speed stays the same. Something must keep pulling the object towards the centre to keep turning it: here, the string. Cut the string and nothing turns the stone any more, so it carries on in a straight line along the tangent, exactly in the direction it was moving at that instant.

🌍 In real life

  • An athlete running round a circular track, a giant wheel at a mela and the tip of a ceiling fan blade all move (nearly) in uniform circular motion.
  • In the hammer throw, the athlete whirls the hammer round and then lets go; it flies off along the tangent, just like the stone here.
  • Mud flies off a spinning bicycle wheel along the tangent, and sparks leave a grinding wheel in straight tangent lines.
  • The Earth goes round the Sun in a nearly circular orbit (r ≈ 150 million km, T ≈ 365 days) at about 30 km/s; the Sun's gravity is the "string".

  1. Keep r = 1 m and change T from 2 s to 1 s. What happens to the speed?
    Show answerIt doubles, from about 3.14 m/s to 6.28 m/s: the stone covers the same 6.28 m circumference in half the time.
  2. Cut the string when the stone is at the far right (east) of the circle. In which direction does it fly?
    Show answerDue north (up the screen), along the tangent, not outwards away from the centre. At that point the stone was moving north, and with no pull it simply keeps that velocity.
  3. Look at the 8 velocity arrows round the circle. What is the same about them, and what is different?
    Show answerThey all have the same length (the same speed), but each points in a different direction. So the velocity is different at every point, even though the speed is constant.
  4. An athlete runs round a circular track of radius 50 m in 40 s. What is the speed?
    Show answerv = 2πr ÷ T = 2 × 3.14 × 50 ÷ 40 ≈ 7.85 m/s. After one full round the displacement is zero, but the distance is 314 m.
  5. Switch on the Class 11 pull arrow. Double the speed by halving T. What happens to the pull needed?
    Show answera = v² ÷ r, so doubling v makes the acceleration 4 times bigger. The string must pull 4 times harder, which is why a string may snap when you whirl a stone too fast.

📖 Key terms

  • Uniform circular motion: motion in a circle at constant speed.
  • Tangent: a straight line that touches a circle at one point, at right angles to the radius there.
  • Time period T: the time for one complete round.
  • Centripetal ("centre-seeking") force: the pull towards the centre that keeps an object moving in a circle.
