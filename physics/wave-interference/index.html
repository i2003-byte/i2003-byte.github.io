📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

When two waves reach the same place, the disturbances simply add up (the principle of superposition). Where a crest meets a crest, the water rises twice as high: constructive interference. Where a crest meets a trough, they cancel and the water stays calm: destructive interference.

Two dippers moving up and down together are coherent sources: same wavelength and a fixed phase difference. Their ripples make a steady pattern of lively and calm lines that fan out from between the sources. The same idea, with light waves, gives the bright and dark fringes of Young's double-slit experiment.

🧮 Key formulas

bright (big waves): r₂ − r₁ = 0, ±λ, ±2λ, … = m λ calm (no waves): r₂ − r₁ = ±λ/2, ±3λ/2, … = (m + ½) λ

r₂ − r₁ is the path difference: how much farther one wave has travelled. For sources in opposite step (phase π) the two rules swap.

band spacing on a far screen β ≈ λ D ÷ d v = f λ

D is the distance to the screen and d the distance between the sources. Bigger λ or smaller d spreads the pattern out. This formula needs D much bigger than d; the yellow screen at the right always shows the exact pattern.

Simplifications: each wave weakens as 1 ÷ √r as it spreads out in a circle, the edges of the tank do not reflect, and the ripples move at 25 cm/s. The motion is shown slowed down to about one wave a second (a real tank ripples at about 5–25 Hz).

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Two speakers playing the same note: walk across the room and the sound gets loud and quiet in turn.
  • Noise-cancelling headphones play a copy of the outside noise in opposite step, so the two cancel at your ear.
  • The colours of a soap bubble and of oil on a wet road come from light waves reflected from two surfaces interfering.

🎯 Try this

  1. Drag the probe onto the middle line, straight across from between the sources. What is the path difference, and is the water lively or calm there?
    Show answerr₂ − r₁ = 0, so the crests always arrive together: a bright (antinodal) line, the central maximum.
  2. Turn on “Mark bright and calm lines”. Move the probe onto the first dashed line. What is the path difference?
    Show answerAbout ±0.5 λ: one wave travels half a wavelength farther, so a crest from one meets a trough from the other. The water there hardly moves.
  3. With λ = 2 cm, how many bright lines do you expect for d = 6 cm? Check the readout.
    Show answerThe path difference can be at most d = 3λ, so the bright lines are m = 0, ±1, ±2, ±3: 7 lines (the outermost ones run along the line of the sources).
  4. Double the wavelength. Then halve the distance between the sources. What happens to the bands on the screen each time?
    Show answerBoth spread the bands out (β ≈ λD/d gets bigger), and there are fewer bright lines. To see interference clearly with light, whose λ is tiny, the slits must be very close together.
  5. Switch the dippers to “opposite step”. What happens on the middle line?
    Show answerIt becomes calm. A crest from one source now always meets a trough from the other on the middle line, so bright and calm lines swap places.

📖 Key terms

  • Coherent sources: sources with the same frequency and a constant phase difference.
  • Path difference: the difference between the distances travelled by the two waves to a point.
  • Antinodal line: a line of strong waves (constructive interference). Nodal line: a line of calm water (destructive interference).
  • Fringe width (β): the distance between neighbouring bright (or dark) bands on a screen.
