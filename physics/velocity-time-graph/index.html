📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

A velocity–time graph tells the whole story of a trip along a straight road. Its height at any moment is the velocity. Its slope (how steeply it rises or falls) is the acceleration: a line going up means speeding up, a flat line means steady velocity, and a line going down means slowing down (retardation). Best of all, the area under the graph is the distance travelled. That is because distance = velocity × time, and a thin strip under the graph has height v and width Δt, so its area is v × Δt. Adding all the strips gives the total distance. Here the area is cut into a trapezium, a rectangle and a triangle, which are easy to work out.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

acceleration a = slope = (v − u) ÷ t v = u + a t distance = area under the v–t graph trapezium: s = ½ (u + v) t = u t + ½ a t² rectangle: s = v t triangle (stopping): s = ½ v t 1 m/s = 3.6 km/h (72 km/h = 20 m/s)
  • Metro example: 0 → 20 m/s in 20 s (a = 1 m/s²) covers ½ × 20 × 20 = 200 m; 30 s at 20 m/s covers 600 m; braking at 1 m/s² takes 20 s and 200 m. Total 1000 m.
  • A negative slope is a negative acceleration (retardation). The area is still positive because the velocity is still forward.

Simplification: each phase has a constant acceleration, and the changes between phases are instant. Real vehicles change their acceleration smoothly. The trip is sped up so that it plays in about 12 s on screen; the readouts use real time.

🌍 In real life

  • A metro train between two stations about 1 km apart follows almost exactly this shape: speed up, run steady, brake smoothly into the next platform.
  • Stopping distance of a car = reaction distance (the flat part, before the brakes work) + braking distance (the triangle). This is why road-safety campaigns ask drivers to keep a gap.
  • Speed data from a GPS phone app can be turned into a v–t graph, and its area gives the distance of your run or cycle ride.

📖 Key terms

  • Velocity: speed in a stated direction. Uniform velocity: the same speed and direction all the time.
  • Acceleration: the rate of change of velocity, in m/s² (metres per second, every second).
  • Retardation (deceleration): acceleration that slows the object down.
  • Uniformly accelerated motion: motion with constant acceleration; its v–t graph is a straight line.
