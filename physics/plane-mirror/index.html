📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

A mirror sends light back so that the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, both measured from the normal (a line at 90° to the mirror). When the reflected light enters your eye, your brain traces it back in a straight line, so the object seems to be behind the mirror. That point is the image.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

angle i = angle r

the incident ray, the reflected ray and the normal all lie in the same plane

  • The image is as far behind the mirror as the object is in front.
  • The image is virtual (no light really reaches it, so it can't be caught on a screen), upright and the same size.
  • Left and right are swapped: lateral inversion. Your right hand looks like a left hand in the mirror.

The diagram is a top view: you are looking down on the mirror, which is seen edge-on as a line.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • AMBULANCE is painted in mirror writing on the front of ambulances so drivers ahead read it the right way in their rear-view mirrors.
  • Barbers put a mirror behind you and one in front so you can see the back of your head.
  • A periscope uses two plane mirrors to see over a wall.

🎯 Try this

  1. Drag the eye to different places. Do i and r ever differ?
    Show answerNo. Wherever the eye is, the light that reaches it has i = r.
  2. Move the object from 40 cm to 70 cm from the mirror. Where is the image now?
    Show answer70 cm behind the mirror. Image distance always equals object distance.
  3. Turn on "Show many rays". Where do the dashed lines meet?
    Show answerAll the reflected rays, traced back, meet at one point behind the mirror: the image I. That's why every eye position sees the image in the same place.
  4. Pick MOM, then TOYOTA. Why do they still read nicely in the mirror?
    Show answerEach of their letters is symmetric about a vertical line, so flipping a letter doesn't change it. MOM reads the same backwards too; TOYOTA becomes ATOYOT.
  5. Make the mirror short and put the eye far to one side. Why does the message say the eye can't see the image?
    Show answerThe light would have to bounce off a point beyond the mirror's edge. No mirror there, so no reflected ray reaches the eye.

📖 Key terms

  • Normal: a line at right angles to the mirror at the point where light hits it.
  • Virtual image: an image from which light only seems to come; it can't be caught on a screen.
  • Lateral inversion: the swapping of left and right in a mirror image.
