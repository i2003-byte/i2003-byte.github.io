📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Our voice is produced in the larynx (voice box) at the top of the windpipe. Two vocal cords stretch across it, leaving a narrow slit. When air from the lungs is forced through the slit, the vocal cords vibrate and produce sound. Muscles make the cords tighter or looser, changing the pitch. Men have longer cords (about 20 mm) than women (about 15 mm) and children, so men usually have deeper voices.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

Tighter & thinner cords → higher pitch Longer & thicker cords → lower pitch More air pushed → louder voice

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Put your fingers on your throat and hum — you can feel the vibration
  • A boy’s voice “breaks” (gets deeper) as his vocal cords grow
  • Stretching a rubber band tighter gives a higher sound when plucked — the same idea

🎯 Try this

  1. Keep the tightness the same and switch between man, woman and child. Which is highest?
    Show answerThe child — shortest vocal cords give the highest pitch.
  2. Set the air to 0%. Is there any sound?
    Show answerNo — without air flowing, the cords do not vibrate.
  3. How can a singer reach a high note?
    Show answerBy tightening the vocal cords.

📖 Key terms

  • Larynx — The voice box, at the upper end of the windpipe.
  • Vocal cords — Two folds in the larynx that vibrate to produce voice.
  • Windpipe — The tube that carries air between the throat and the lungs.
