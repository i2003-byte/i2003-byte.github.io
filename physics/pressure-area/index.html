📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Pressure is how concentrated a force is: the force acting on each square metre of surface. The same brick pushes on the sand with the same force (its weight) whichever face is down, but on a small face that force is squeezed into a small area, so the pressure is bigger and the brick sinks deeper.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

pressure = force ÷ area

P in pascal (Pa) = newton per square metre (N/m²) · 1 cm² = 0.0001 m²

weight F = m × g

a 3 kg brick weighs 3 × 9.8 = 29.4 N

  • Same force, smaller area → more pressure.
  • Same area, more force → more pressure.

Simplification: the brick is 23 × 11 × 7 cm with a mass of 3 kg. The dent depth uses a simple rule (depth in mm = P ÷ 300); real sand firms up as it is squeezed.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • School bags have wide straps so the weight spreads over more of your shoulder and hurts less.
  • A sharp knife has a very thin edge (tiny area), so a small push gives a huge pressure and cuts vegetables easily.
  • Camels have broad feet so they don't sink in the sand of the Thar desert.
  • Nails and drawing pins have pointed tips to go into wood or a board easily.

🎯 Try this

  1. With one brick, work out the pressure on the largest face (253 cm²).
    Show answerArea = 0.0253 m². P = 29.4 ÷ 0.0253 ≈ 1162 Pa.
  2. Stand the brick on its smallest end. How many times bigger is the pressure than on the largest face?
    Show answer253 ÷ 77 ≈ 3.3 times. The force is the same, the area is 3.3 times smaller.
  3. Stack 2 bricks on the largest face. Is that more or less pressure than 1 brick on its end?
    Show answerLess: 2 bricks flat give about 2324 Pa, 1 brick on its end gives about 3818 Pa. Area matters a lot.
  4. Why is it easier to walk on soft sand in flat sandals than in narrow heels?
    Show answerFlat sandals spread your weight over a larger area, so the pressure is lower and you sink less.

📖 Key terms

  • Force: a push or a pull, measured in newtons (N).
  • Thrust: a force acting at right angles to a surface, like the brick's weight on the sand.
  • Pascal (Pa): the SI unit of pressure, 1 N on 1 m².
