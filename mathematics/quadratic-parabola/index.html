💡 The big idea

A quadratic equation ax² + bx + c = 0 (with a ≠ 0) asks: for which x is ax² + bx + c zero? Draw y = ax² + bx + c and you get a U-shaped curve called a parabola. The answers, called roots, are the x-values where the curve meets the x-axis, because there y = 0.

A parabola can cross the x-axis twice, just touch it once, or miss it completely. So a quadratic equation has two, one or no real roots. The discriminant D = b² − 4ac tells you which, without drawing anything.

🧮 Key formulas

x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / 2a

The quadratic formula (Sridharacharya’s formula). The ± gives the two roots, one on each side of the axis of symmetry.

D = b² − 4ac
  • D > 0: two different real roots; the curve crosses the x-axis twice.
  • D = 0: two equal roots x = −b/2a; the curve just touches the x-axis at its vertex.
  • D < 0: no real roots; the curve stays above (a > 0) or below (a < 0) the x-axis.
vertex = (−b/2a, −D/4a) axis of symmetry: x = −b/2a sum of roots = −b/a product of roots = c/a

🌍 Real-world examples

  • A ball or a stream of water from a garden pipe follows a parabola. Its height is a quadratic in time, so “when does it hit the ground?” is a quadratic equation.
  • A farmer with 40 m of fencing wants a rectangular plot of 96 m²: x(20 − x) = 96 gives x² − 20x + 96 = 0, so the plot is 8 m by 12 m.
  • Dish antennas and car headlight mirrors are parabola-shaped, because a parabola sends parallel rays to a single point.

    Show answerAt x = 2 and x = 3. D = 25 − 24 = 1, so x = (5 ± 1)/2 = 3 or 2. Their sum is 5 = −b/a and product 6 = c/a.

    Show answerThe parabola flips and opens downwards. With x² − 5x + 6 → −x² − 5x + 6, D = 25 + 24 = 49 and the roots become −6 and 1. The vertex is now the highest point.

📖 Key terms

  • Parabola: the graph of a quadratic, symmetric about a vertical line.
  • Vertex: the turning point, the lowest or highest point of the parabola.
  • Discriminant: b² − 4ac, which “discriminates” between the kinds of roots.
