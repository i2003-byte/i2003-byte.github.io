📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

In a right-angled triangle, the longest side (opposite the right angle) is called the hypotenuse, c. The other two sides are a and b. Pythagoras' theorem says the square on the hypotenuse has exactly the same area as the two smaller squares put together.

The rearrangement proof needs no algebra. Put four copies of the triangle in a square of side a + b. In the first arrangement the empty space is one tilted square, c². Slide three triangles (without turning them) and the empty space becomes two squares, a² and b². The triangles did not change, so the empty space did not change either.

🧮 Key formulas

c² = a² + b² (right angle between a and b) c = √(a² + b²)

The same proof with numbers: big square (a + b)², minus four triangles 4 × ½ab = 2ab, leaves a² + b².

Pythagorean triples are whole numbers that fit exactly: 3, 4, 5 · 6, 8, 10 · 5, 12, 13 · 9, 12, 15 · 8, 15, 17.

Only for right angles. If the angle between a and b is less than 90°, c² is smaller than a² + b²; if it is more than 90°, c² is bigger. So the theorem can also be used backwards to test whether a corner is a right angle.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Masons mark a right-angled corner for a house with a rope of 3, 4 and 5 units: when the rope is pulled tight into a triangle, the corner between 3 and 4 is 90°.
  • A 13 m ladder with its foot 5 m from a wall reaches 12 m up the wall.
  • A TV screen's size is its diagonal: a screen 80 cm wide and 45 cm high is about 92 cm (36 inches) across.
  • Ancient Indian mathematicians knew this rule: the Baudhāyana Śulba Sūtra (about 800 BCE) describes it for building fire altars.

📖 Key terms

  • Hypotenuse: the side opposite the right angle; always the longest side.
  • Legs: the two sides that make the right angle (a and b).
  • Pythagorean triple: three whole numbers a, b, c with a² + b² = c².
