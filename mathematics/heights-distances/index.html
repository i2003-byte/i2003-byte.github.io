📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

You cannot put a measuring tape up the Qutub Minar, but you can measure two things on the ground: how far you stand from its foot, and the angle you look up to see its top. The line from your eye to the top is the line of sight. The angle between it and the horizontal is the angle of elevation. Distance, height and line of sight make a right triangle, so tan does the rest.

Looking down from a height, the angle below the horizontal is the angle of depression. The horizontal at the top is parallel to the ground, so the angle of depression from the top equals the angle of elevation from the bottom (alternate angles).

🧮 Formulas

tan θ = height ÷ distance ⇒ h = d × tan θ (+ eye height) two points: x + g = h cot α, x = h cot β ⇒ h = g ÷ (cot α − cot β) angle of depression δ from height h: d = h ÷ tan δ

Angles are measured from the eye, so add the eye height (about 1.5 m) to the answer, or take it away from the height in the formula. A school clinometer reads to the nearest degree; that small error is why the worked-out answer is a little off. Simplified: flat ground, the top seen exactly above the foot, heights rounded (Qutub Minar 72.5 m, Charminar 56 m, an 18 m coconut tree, a lighthouse lamp 60 m above the sea). People and the boat are drawn larger than scale.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • In 1852 the Great Trigonometrical Survey of India worked out the height of Peak XV (Mount Everest) from angles measured from plains more than 100 km away.
  • You can make a clinometer from a protractor, a straw and a thread with a weight, and measure your school building.
  • Lighthouse keepers and coast guards estimate the distance of ships from the angle of depression.
  • Pilots use the angle of the glide path to land: a 3° approach drops about 52 m for every kilometre.

🎯 Try this

  1. Height of the Qutub Minar: stand 100 m away. What angle do you measure, and what height do you work out?
    Show answerAbout 35° (35.4° exactly). h = 100 × tan 35° + 1.5 = 70.0 + 1.5 = 71.5 m, about 1.4% less than the true 72.5 m, because the clinometer rounds the angle down.
  2. Turn off “whole degrees”. Does the answer get better? Then switch it back on and move closer to the tower. When is the rounding error biggest?
    Show answerWith tenths of a degree the answer is within a few tenths of a metre. With whole degrees the error depends on how much the angle is rounded. Half a degree matters most when the angle is very steep (close to the tower, where tan changes fast) or very shallow (far away). Angles near 45° give the most reliable answers.
  3. Coconut tree: at what distance is the angle of elevation 45°? Why is that a handy distance?
    Show answerBetween 16 and 17 m: exactly 16.5 m, because the tree is 18 m and the eye 1.5 m high. At 45°, tan = 1, so the height above your eye equals your distance from the tree: no calculator needed.
  4. Charminar from two points: you measure α from P, walk 50 m closer to Q and measure β. Why do we need two angles?
    Show answerWhen you can't reach the foot (a crowd or a river is in the way), you don't know x. Two angles and the gap give two equations, and subtracting them removes x.
  5. Lighthouse: move the boat from 150 m to 50 m. What happens to the angle of depression?
    Show answerIt grows from about 22° to about 50°. The closer the boat, the steeper you look down. The angle of elevation from the boat to the lamp is the same angle.

📖 Key terms

  • Line of sight: the line from the observer's eye to the object.
  • Angle of elevation: the angle above the horizontal when looking up at an object.
  • Angle of depression: the angle below the horizontal when looking down at an object.
  • Clinometer: a simple instrument for measuring angles of elevation.
