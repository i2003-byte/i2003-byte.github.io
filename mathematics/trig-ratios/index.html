📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

In a right-angled triangle, pick one of the two sharp (acute) angles and call it θ. The three sides then get names from where they sit: the hypotenuse is the longest side, across from the right angle; the opposite side is across from θ; the adjacent side is the other side touching θ.

Divide one side by another and you get a trigonometric ratio. The surprise: for a given angle the ratio is always the same, however big or small the triangle is, because all right triangles with that angle are similar (same shape, different size). So a ratio like sin 30° is just a number: 0.5.

🧮 Formulas

sin θ = opposite ÷ hypotenuse cos θ = adjacent ÷ hypotenuse tan θ = opposite ÷ adjacent cosec θ = 1 ÷ sin θ sec θ = 1 ÷ cos θ cot θ = 1 ÷ tan θ tan θ = sin θ ÷ cos θ sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 sin(90° − θ) = cos θ tan(90° − θ) = cot θ

Standard values: sin 30° = 1/2, sin 45° = 1/√2, sin 60° = √3/2; cos goes the other way (cos 30° = √3/2, cos 60° = 1/2); tan 30° = 1/√3, tan 45° = 1, tan 60° = √3. A memory trick: one old way is “Some People Have Curly Brown Hair Through Proper Brushing” (sin = P/H, cos = B/H, tan = P/B, with P = perpendicular, B = base, H = hypotenuse).

Lengths are shown to 2 decimal places and ratios to 3, so a calculation with the rounded lengths can differ in the last digit.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • A ramp for wheelchairs should rise gently: a slope of 1 in 12 means tan θ = 1/12, an angle of about 4.8°.
  • Surveyors and engineers use tan to find the height of hills, towers and bridges they cannot climb.
  • A ladder leaning against a wall: if it makes 60° with the ground, its foot is cos 60° = half its length from the wall.
  • Roofs, staircases and road slopes in hill stations are designed with these ratios.

📖 Key terms

  • Hypotenuse: the side opposite the right angle; the longest side.
  • Opposite / adjacent: the side facing the chosen angle / the shorter side touching it.
  • Similar triangles: triangles with the same angles; their sides are in the same ratio.
  • Complementary angles: two angles that add up to 90°, like the two acute angles of a right triangle.
