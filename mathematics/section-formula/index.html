📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Cut the segment AB into m + n equal steps. The point P that is m steps from A and n steps from B divides AB in the ratio m : n.

Look at the coloured bars on the axes: the x-coordinates and the y-coordinates are split in exactly the same ratio. That is because the small right triangle from A to P and the one from P to B have the same shape (they are similar), only different sizes. So you can work on x and y separately, and each is a weighted average of the two ends.

🧮 Key formulas

P = ( (m·x₂ + n·x₁)/(m + n) , (m·y₂ + n·y₁)/(m + n) )

Section formula. A(x₁, y₁), B(x₂, y₂), AP : PB = m : n. Notice the cross-over: m (the A side) multiplies the coordinates of B. The bigger m is, the closer P gets to B.

Midpoint M = ( (x₁ + x₂)/2 , (y₁ + y₂)/2 )

The ratio 1 : 1. The midpoint is the plain average of the two ends.

Centroid G = ( (x₁ + x₂ + x₃)/3 , (y₁ + y₂ + y₃)/3 )

A median joins a corner to the midpoint of the opposite side. The three medians meet at G, which divides each median 2 : 1 from the corner.

Points of trisection cut AB into three equal parts: they divide it 1 : 2 and 2 : 1.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Planting saplings along a straight road: to place a tree one-third of the way from one milestone to the next, use the ratio 1 : 2.
  • A cardboard triangle balances on a pencil tip placed at its centroid. That is why the centroid is also called the centre of mass of a thin, even triangle.
  • A see-saw with a heavy child and a light child balances when the pivot divides the plank in the inverse ratio of their weights, the same weighted average as the section formula.

🎯 Try this

  1. With A(−6, −3) and B(6, 5), set m = 3 and n = 1. Where is P? Now set m = 1 and n = 3. What changed?
    Show answer3 : 1 gives P(3, 3), close to B. 1 : 3 gives P(−3, −1), close to A. Swapping the ratio mirrors P along the segment.
  2. Set m = 2 and n = 2, then m = 1 and n = 1. Compare the two points.
    Show answerBoth are the midpoint (0, 1). Only the ratio matters: 2 : 2 is the same as 1 : 1.
  3. Move A to (−2, 1) and B to (6, 5). Find m : n so that P lands on the y-axis.
    Show answer1 : 3. On the y-axis x = 0, so (6m − 2n)/(m + n) = 0, giving 6m = 2n. Then P = (0, 2).
  4. Choose “Points of trisection”. Check that Q is the midpoint of P and B.
    Show answerWith A(−6, −3), B(6, 5): P(−2, −1/3) and Q(2, 7/3). The midpoint of P and B is ((−2 + 6)/2, (−1/3 + 5)/2) = (2, 7/3) = Q.
  5. Choose “Centroid of triangle ABC” and drag C around. Does AG ÷ GD stay the same?
    Show answerYes, always 2. G is two-thirds of the way along every median, wherever the corners are.

📖 Key terms

  • Divides internally: P lies between A and B (the only case in this sim).
  • Median: a line from a corner of a triangle to the midpoint of the opposite side.
  • Centroid: the point where the three medians meet.
