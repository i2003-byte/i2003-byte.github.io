📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

A graph shows where two lines cross, but it can only give a rough answer when the crossing is at a point such as (25/11, 1/11). Algebra gives the exact answer. The trick is always the same: turn two equations with two unknowns into one equation with one unknown, solve it, then go back for the other letter.

Think of every equation as a balance that is level: the left side weighs the same as the right side. You may do anything you like to it, as long as you do the same thing to both pans: add, subtract, multiply or divide by the same (non-zero) number. It stays level, so it is still true.

  • Substitution: make one letter the subject of one equation (y = 14 − x), then put that expression into the other equation in place of the letter. Swapping something for an equal amount keeps the balance level.
  • Elimination: multiply the equations so one letter has the same number in front in both (the LCM), then add or subtract the two equations so that letter cancels. Adding equal amounts to both pans keeps a balance level, and the two sides of an equation are equal amounts.

If both letters vanish, look at what is left. 0 = 0 is always true: the equations are the same line, with infinitely many solutions. 0 = 4 is never true: the lines are parallel and there is no solution.

🧮 Key formulas

a₁x + b₁y = c₁ ① a₂x + b₂y = c₂ ② Substitution: from ①, y = (c₁ − a₁x)/b₁, then put it into ② Elimination: ① × m₁ and ② × m₂ so the y terms match, then ① ± ②

Example: 3x + 2y = 7 and x − 3y = 2. Multiply ② by 3: 3x − 9y = 6. Subtract from ①: 11y = 1, so y = 1/11. Then 3x + 2/11 = 7 gives x = 25/11.

Ends in 0 = 0 → infinitely many Ends in 0 = k (k ≠ 0) → none

The sim picks the easiest route for you: for substitution, the letter whose number in front is smallest (best of all 1 or −1); for elimination, the letter whose LCM is smaller. Any other choice gives the same answer, just with harder numbers. All working is done with exact fractions, never decimals.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • A shop sells 2 kg of rice and 3 kg of dal for ₹370, and 3 kg of rice and 1 kg of dal for ₹240. Elimination finds both prices without guessing.
  • Mixing two fertilisers, two medicines or two alloys to get an exact amount of each ingredient is a pair of linear equations.
  • Computers use elimination (in a form called Gaussian elimination) to solve thousands of equations at once, for example to work out the currents in an electric grid or the forces in a bridge.
  • Balancing a chemical equation can be set up and solved the same way: one equation for each kind of atom.

📖 Key terms

  • Substitution method: solve one equation for one letter and put that expression into the other equation.
  • Elimination method: make the numbers in front of one letter equal, then add or subtract the equations to remove it.
  • Coefficient: the number in front of a letter, like 3 in 3x.
  • LCM: least common multiple; the smallest number both coefficients divide into, used to make them match.
  • Back-substitution: putting the value you found into an earlier equation to get the other letter.
