📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Volume is the amount of space a solid takes up. We measure it by counting how many unit cubes fit inside. A cube with 1 cm edges has a volume of 1 cubic centimetre (1 cm³).

A cuboid that is l cm long and b cm broad holds l × b cubes in its bottom layer. If it is h cm high there are h such layers, so it holds l × b × h cubes. That is why the volume is length × breadth × height. Capacity is how much a container can hold, usually measured in litres (L) and millilitres (mL). A box of 1000 cm³ (10 cm × 10 cm × 10 cm) holds exactly 1 litre.

🧮 Formulas

Cuboid: V = l × b × h

Area of the base (l × b) times the height. All three lengths must be in the same unit.

Cube of edge a: V = a × a × a = a³

Double the edge and the volume becomes 2 × 2 × 2 = 8 times bigger.

Cylinder: V = πr²h

Again area of the base (a circle, πr²) times the height. The radius r is half the diameter. Take π ≈ 22/7 or 3.14.

1 cm³ = 1 mL · 1000 cm³ = 1 L · 1 m³ = 1000 L

1 m = 100 cm, so 1 m³ = 100 × 100 × 100 cm³ = 10,00,000 cm³ = 1000 L.

Simplifications: the containers have walls of zero thickness, so the inside and outside sizes are the same. The jug pours a steady 250 mL (one glass) each second. The plastic tank sizes are rounded typical sizes, so a “1000 L” tank works out to about 998 L. The family uses water at a steady rate all day and night (really most is used in the morning and evening), and the tank is not refilled. The 135 L and 55 L per person per day figures are rounded planning guidelines for Indian cities and villages; real use varies a lot. Tanks are drawn see-through so you can watch the level.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Milk packets hold 500 mL = 500 cm³; a one-litre Tetra Pak is a cuboid of roughly 6.5 cm × 9.5 cm × 16.5 cm, close to 1000 cm³.
  • Rooftop tanks: plastic tanks on Indian houses come in sizes like 500 L, 1000 L and 2000 L. Builders pick the size from the number of people and the water each uses in a day.
  • Bricks: a standard brick is about 19 cm × 9 cm × 9 cm = 1539 cm³. Dividing the volume of a wall by the volume of one brick tells a mason roughly how many bricks to buy.
  • Rain on a roof: 1 cm of rain on a 10 m × 10 m roof is 10 m × 10 m × 0.01 m = 1 m³ = 1000 L, enough to fill a big tank. This is the idea behind rainwater harvesting.

📖 Key terms

  • Volume: the space a solid occupies, in cubic units such as cm³ or m³.
  • Capacity: the amount a hollow container can hold, usually in litres or millilitres.
  • Unit cube: a cube with edges of 1 unit; its volume is 1 cubic unit.
  • Cuboid: a solid with six rectangular faces, like a brick or a matchbox.
  • Cylinder: a solid with two equal circular ends joined by a curved surface, like a tin can or a pipe.
