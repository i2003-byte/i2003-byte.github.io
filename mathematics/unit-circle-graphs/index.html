📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Draw a circle of radius 1 around the origin and let a point P go round it. Join P to the centre: the radius makes an angle θ with the positive x-axis. Drop a line from P to the x-axis and you get a right triangle with hypotenuse 1. So the height of P is sin θ and the distance across to P is cos θ: P is the point (cos θ, sin θ).

This lets sin and cos be defined for any angle, not only angles inside a triangle. As P goes round, its height rises and falls, tracing the smooth sine wave; the cosine wave is the same shape shifted by 90°. They repeat every 360°.

🧮 Formulas

P = (cos θ, sin θ) sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 tan θ = sin θ ÷ cos θ (not defined at 90° and 270°) sin(θ + 360°) = sin θ cos(90° − θ) = sin θ

Signs by quadrant: in I all are positive, in II only sin, in III only tan, in IV only cos. Class 10 uses angles from 0° to 90° (shaded green on the graph); angles beyond 90° and the full graphs are Class 11 work.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • The height of a seat on a giant wheel at a mela goes up and down as a sine wave in time.
  • Household AC electricity in India changes direction 50 times a second; its voltage follows a sine wave.
  • Sound waves, tides and the swing of a pendulum all follow sine-shaped patterns.

🎯 Try this

  1. Press Go round and watch the sine graph. At which angles is sin θ largest, smallest and zero?
    Show answerLargest (1) at 90°, smallest (−1) at 270°, zero at 0°, 180° and 360°, where P sits on the x-axis.
  2. Choose “sin θ and cos θ together”. Where do the two curves cross between 0° and 90°?
    Show answerAt 45°, where sin 45° = cos 45° = 1/√2 ≈ 0.707. P is then exactly halfway between the axes.
  3. Drag P from 0° to 90°. How do sin θ and cos θ change?
    Show answersin θ grows from 0 to 1 while cos θ falls from 1 to 0. At 30° sin is 0.5; at 60° cos is 0.5.
  4. Choose y = tan θ and move P towards 90°. What happens to the tangent line and the graph?
    Show answerThe extended radius meets the line x = 1 higher and higher; at 90° it is parallel to that line and never meets it. tan θ grows without limit and is not defined at 90°.
  5. Set θ = 150°. Compare sin 150° with sin 30°, and cos 150° with cos 30°.
    Show answersin 150° = sin 30° = 0.5 (same height), but cos 150° = −cos 30° ≈ −0.866 because P is now on the left of the y-axis.

📖 Key terms

  • Unit circle: a circle of radius 1 centred at the origin.
  • Quadrant: one of the four quarters of the plane made by the axes.
  • Period: how far you go before a graph repeats (360° for sin and cos, 180° for tan).
