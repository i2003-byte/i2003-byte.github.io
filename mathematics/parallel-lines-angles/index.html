📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

A transversal is a line that cuts two other lines. At each crossing it makes four angles, so there are eight angles in all, numbered 1 to 8 here (1 to 4 at P, 5 to 8 at Q).

When the two lines are parallel, the transversal meets both of them at the same slant. That is why the angles at Q are exact copies of the angles at P. Tilt line m a little and the copies stop matching. This is how we test whether two lines are parallel.

🧮 Key rules

Corresponding angles are equal (∠1 = ∠5, ∠2 = ∠6, ∠3 = ∠7, ∠4 = ∠8) Alternate interior angles are equal (∠3 = ∠5, ∠4 = ∠6) Co-interior angles add up to 180° (∠4 + ∠5 = 180°, ∠3 + ∠6 = 180°)

These three rules hold only when l ∥ m. The reverse is also true: if any one of them holds, the lines are parallel.

Vertically opposite angles are equal · a linear pair adds up to 180°

These two work at any crossing of two lines, parallel or not.

Interior angles lie between the two lines (3, 4, 5, 6). Exterior angles lie outside them (1, 2, 7, 8). Alternate means on opposite sides of the transversal.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Railway tracks are parallel. A road crossing them at a slant makes equal corresponding angles at both rails.
  • A zebra crossing, the rungs of a ladder and the lines on a ruled notebook page are all parallel lines cut by a transversal.
  • Carpenters and masons check that two edges are parallel by measuring the angles a crossing line makes with each of them.

🎯 Try this

  1. Keep line m at 0° tilt and set the transversal to 60°. Find the sizes of all eight angles. How many different sizes are there?
    Show answerOnly two: 60° (∠1, ∠3, ∠5, ∠7) and 120° (∠2, ∠4, ∠6, ∠8). Turn on “Show all eight sizes” to check. 60° + 120° = 180°.
  2. Choose “Alternate interior angles” and tap ∠3. Now tilt line m to 10°. What happens?
    Show answer∠3 stays 60° but ∠5 becomes 50°, so they are no longer equal. Unequal alternate angles mean the lines are not parallel.
  3. Choose “Co-interior angles” with the lines parallel. Move the transversal. Is the sum ever anything other than 180°?
    Show answerNo. For parallel lines, co-interior angles always add up to 180°, whatever the slant of the transversal.
  4. Tilt line m to −15° and choose “Vertically opposite angles”. Does the rule still work?
    Show answerYes. Vertically opposite angles are equal at any crossing, because both are “180° minus the same angle”. Parallel lines are not needed.
  5. Set the transversal to 90°. What can you say about all eight angles?
    Show answerAll eight are right angles (90°). The transversal is perpendicular to both parallel lines.

📖 Key terms

  • Transversal: a line that crosses two or more lines at different points.
  • Parallel lines (l ∥ m): lines in a plane that never meet, however far they are drawn.
  • Linear pair: two angles side by side that make a straight line (180°).
