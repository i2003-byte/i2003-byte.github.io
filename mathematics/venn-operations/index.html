💡 The big idea

Two circles inside a rectangle split the universal set U into four parts: only A, both A and B, only B, and neither. Every set you can make from A and B with union, intersection, difference and complement is just a choice of some of these four parts. That gives exactly 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 16 possible sets, and the sim has a name for each one.

So instead of memorising rules, shade the regions. If two expressions shade the same regions, they are the same set. That is how De Morgan’s laws can be seen at a glance.

🧮 Key formulas

A ∪ B = {x : x ∈ A or x ∈ B} A ∩ B = {x : x ∈ A and x ∈ B} A − B = {x : x ∈ A and x ∉ B} A′ = U − A n(A ∪ B) = n(A) + n(B) − n(A ∩ B)

Adding n(A) and n(B) counts the overlap twice, so take it away once.

(A ∪ B)′ = A′ ∩ B′ (A ∩ B)′ = A′ ∪ B′

De Morgan’s laws. Also n(A′) = n(U) − n(A), and n(A − B) = n(A) − n(A ∩ B).

🌍 Real-world examples

  • A web search for “mango OR pickle” finds the union of two sets of pages; “mango AND pickle” finds the intersection; “mango −pickle” finds the difference.
  • A railway enquiry: trains from Delhi to Mumbai that run daily and have a pantry car is the intersection of two sets of trains.
  • Exam results: students who passed neither Maths nor Science are (M ∪ S)′, the region outside both circles.

🎯 Try this

  1. With A even and B multiples of 3 in U = {1, …, 20}, shade A ∪ B. Check the formula n(A ∪ B) = n(A) + n(B) − n(A ∩ B).
    Show answern(A) = 10, n(B) = 6, n(A ∩ B) = 3 (6, 12, 18), so n(A ∪ B) = 10 + 6 − 3 = 13. Counting the shaded numbers also gives 13.
  2. Choose (A ∪ B)′ and note which regions are shaded. Now choose A′ and then B′. Which regions do A′ and B′ share?
    Show answer(A ∪ B)′ is only the outside region. A′ is only B plus outside; B′ is only A plus outside. They share just the outside region, so A′ ∩ B′ = (A ∪ B)′. That is De Morgan’s law.
  3. Tap the regions to shade only A and only B, but not the middle. What does the sim call this set?
    Show answer(A − B) ∪ (B − A), the elements in exactly one of the two sets (the symmetric difference). It equals (A ∪ B) − (A ∩ B).
  4. Choose “A multiples of 4, B even”. Which region is empty, and what is A − B?
    Show answerThe “only A” region is empty because every multiple of 4 is even (A ⊂ B). So A − B = ∅ and A ∪ B = B, A ∩ B = A.
  5. Choose “A multiples of 5, B squares” and shade A ∩ B. What do you get, and what does the formula become?
    Show answerA ∩ B = ∅ for U up to 20 (the sets are disjoint), so n(A ∪ B) = n(A) + n(B). Now set n to 25: 25 is both a multiple of 5 and a square, so they are no longer disjoint!

📖 Key terms

  • Union (∪): everything in at least one of the sets.
  • Intersection (∩): only what is in both sets.
  • Difference (A − B): what is in A but not in B.
  • Complement (A′): everything in U that is not in A.
