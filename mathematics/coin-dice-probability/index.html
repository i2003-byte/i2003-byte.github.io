💡 The big idea

When you toss a coin you cannot say whether it will land Head or Tail. But toss it many times and about half the tosses are Heads. Probability puts a number on “how likely”.

The experimental (empirical) probability comes from actually doing the trials. The theoretical probability comes from counting equally likely outcomes. With a few trials the two can be far apart; with thousands of trials they get very close.

🧮 Key formulas

experimental P(E) = number of trials in which E happened ÷ total number of trials theoretical P(E) = number of outcomes favourable to E ÷ number of possible outcomes

Only when all outcomes are equally likely. Every probability is between 0 (impossible) and 1 (certain).

Two coins: HH, HT, TH, TT are equally likely, so P(exactly one Head) = 2/4 = 1/2, but P(two Heads) = 1/4. Two dice: 36 equally likely pairs; 6 of them add up to 7, so P(sum 7) = 6/36 = 1/6, the most likely sum.

The computer's random numbers act as a perfectly fair coin and die.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • The toss at the start of a cricket match is fair because P(Head) = P(Tail) = 1/2.
  • Insurance companies use experimental probability from thousands of past cases to set premiums.
  • A weather forecast of “70% chance of rain” means rain fell on about 70 of 100 similar past days.

🎯 Try this

  1. Toss one coin 10 times. Did you get exactly 5 Heads? Clear it (the reset button) and try again a few times.
    Show answerUsually not exactly 5: 3, 4, 6 or 7 Heads are common. In only 10 tosses the experimental probability often differs from 0.5 by 0.1 or more.
  2. Now press “Toss 1000 times” a few times. How close does the experimental probability get to 0.5?
    Show answerWithin about 0.01 to 0.02 after a few thousand tosses. The graph wobbles a lot at first and then flattens out near the theory line.
  3. Throw one die 600 times. About how many 6s do you expect?
    Show answerAbout 600 × 1/6 = 100. You will usually get between 85 and 115.
  4. Toss two coins many times. Is “0 Heads, 1 Head, 2 Heads” a fair three-way split?
    Show answerNo. “1 Head” comes up about half the time, because it can happen in two ways (HT and TH). 0 Heads and 2 Heads each come up about a quarter of the time.
  5. Throw two dice 1000 times. Which sum comes up most, and which least?
    Show answer7 is most common (6 ways out of 36). 2 and 12 are least common (1 way each, 1/36 ≈ 0.028).

📖 Key terms

  • Trial: one toss or throw. Outcome: what can happen in one trial.
  • Event: one or more outcomes we are interested in, like “a 6” or “sum is 7”.
  • Equally likely: no outcome is more likely than another, as for a fair coin or die.
