📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

A long list of numbers is hard to read. Group the values into classes (like 40–50, 50–60) and count how many fall in each: that count is the frequency. A histogram draws each class as a bar touching its neighbours, so you can see at a glance where most values lie.

A frequency polygon joins the mid-points of the bar tops with straight lines. It shows the same shape, and two polygons can be drawn on one graph to compare two sets of data.

🧮 Key rules

class mark = (lower limit + upper limit) ÷ 2

The polygon joins the points (class mark, frequency). It is closed at the class marks of the empty classes just before the first class and just after the last one.

adjusted frequency = (smallest class width ÷ class width) × frequency

Only for unequal class widths: then the area of a bar, not its height, must show the frequency.

Here a value on a boundary goes in the higher class (40 goes in 40–50), except the largest value, which stays in the last class. The bars touch because the classes follow on with no gaps. When the axis does not start at 0, a small zig-zag (a “kink”) on the axis shows that a part has been left out.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Schools draw histograms of marks to see how a whole class did in a test, not just the topper.
  • The India Meteorological Department groups daily temperatures to show how many very hot days a city had.
  • Doctors compare a child's height with the frequency polygon of heights for that age.

🎯 Try this

  1. With the maths test marks and medium classes, tap the tallest bar. Which class has the most students, and what is its class mark?
    Show answer60–70, with 9 students. Its class mark is (60 + 70) ÷ 2 = 65.
  2. Switch between wide, medium and narrow classes. Which picture shows the shape of the data best?
    Show answerMedium classes. Wide classes hide the detail (only 5 bars); narrow classes give many tiny, uneven bars that make the shape hard to see. Usually 5 to 12 classes work well.
  3. Choose “Frequency polygon” only. Where does the polygon start and end, and why?
    Show answerOn the axis, at the class marks of the empty classes just outside the data (for marks with width 10: at −5 and 105). This closes the polygon so its area matches the histogram's.
  4. Turn on “Merge the end classes” and then turn “Adjust heights” off. What goes wrong?
    Show answerThe merged class 80–100 has 9 students. Drawn 9 high and twice as wide as the others, its area looks like 18 students, so it seems to beat 60–70 (9 students). With adjustment its height becomes 9 × 10 ÷ 20 = 4.5, and its area is fair again.
  5. Pick the Delhi temperatures with narrow classes. On how many days did it reach 44 °C or more?
    Show answerAdd the last four classes: 44–45 (3), 45–46 (2), 46–47 (1), 47–48 (0) = 6 days.

📖 Key terms

  • Class interval: a group such as 40–50. Its width is upper limit − lower limit.
  • Frequency: how many values fall in a class. The total of all frequencies is the number of values.
  • Tally marks: strokes in groups of five (four lines and a cross line) used while counting.
