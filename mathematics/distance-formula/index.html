📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Every point on the plane has an address (x, y). To find how far apart two points are, go across from one and then up (or down) to the other. The across step and the up step are the two short sides of a right triangle, and the distance is its longest side. So the distance formula is just Pythagoras’ theorem written with coordinates.

Once you can find lengths, you can test shapes: equal sides, a right angle (Pythagoras again), or three points that lie on one line.

🧮 Key formulas

AB = √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²)

A(x₁, y₁) and B(x₂, y₂). The order does not matter, because squaring removes the minus sign.

OP = √(x² + y²)

Distance of P(x, y) from the origin O(0, 0).

  • Right-angled triangle: the squares of the two shorter sides add up to the square of the longest side.
  • Collinear points: the two shorter distances add up to the longest one (AB + BC = AC). There is no triangle.
  • Square: four equal sides and equal diagonals. Rhombus: four equal sides, diagonals not equal. Rectangle: opposite sides equal and equal diagonals. Parallelogram: opposite sides equal.

Tip: compare the squares of the lengths (AB² = 50) instead of the roots (7.07…). They are whole numbers here, so the tests are exact. The sim does the same.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • A city map with a grid of streets: a shop 3 blocks east and 4 blocks north is 5 blocks away “as the crow flies”, but 7 blocks by road.
  • Phones and GPS find the straight-line distance between two places from their coordinates, the same way (on a curved Earth the formula is a little different).
  • In a computer game, the program checks whether a ball hits a player by comparing the distance between their centres with the sum of their sizes.

🎯 Try this

  1. Start with “Two points”. Move A to (0, 0) and B to (3, 4). How far is B from the origin?
    Show answer√(3² + 4²) = √25 = 5 units. The famous 3-4-5 right triangle.
  2. Move B to (5, −2), so that A(−3, −2) and B are on the same horizontal line. What happens to the formula?
    Show answerΔy = 0, so AB = √(8²) = 8. For points on a horizontal line the distance is just the difference of the x-coordinates.
  3. Choose “Triangle: is it right-angled?”. Which angle is 90°, and how do the squared sides show it?
    Show answerAB² = 36, CA² = 64 and BC² = 100. 36 + 64 = 100, so the triangle is right-angled at A, the corner opposite the longest side BC.
  4. Choose “Three points on a line?”. Check that AB + BC = CA.
    Show answerAB = 3√5, BC = 2√5, CA = 5√5, and 3√5 + 2√5 = 5√5. The points are collinear. Now move C one step: the sum is no longer equal and a thin triangle appears.
  5. Choose “Four points: a rhombus”. Move B to (0, −4) and D to (0, 4). What shape is it now, and why?
    Show answerA square. All four sides are √32 = 4√2 and both diagonals are now 8, so the diagonals are equal too.
  6. Hard: try to make an equilateral triangle with whole-number corners. Can you?
    Show answerNever exactly. A triangle with whole-number corners has an area that is a whole number or a half, but an equilateral triangle with side² a whole number has area (√3/4) × side², which is never a fraction like that. You can only get close, for example sides √64, √65, √65.

📖 Key terms

  • Coordinates (x, y): how far a point is across (x) and up (y) from the origin.
  • Collinear: lying on one straight line.
  • Diagonal: a line joining opposite corners of a quadrilateral (AC and BD).
