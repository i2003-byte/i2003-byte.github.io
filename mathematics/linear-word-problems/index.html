📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Many everyday puzzles have two unknowns: the price of a notebook and of a pen, a mother's age and her son's, a boat's speed and the river's. One fact is not enough to find two numbers, but two different facts are. Each fact becomes a linear equation, and the answer is the pair of numbers that makes both true.

To solve by graph, as in your notebook:

  1. Choose letters for the unknowns (say x and y) and write what each means, with units.
  2. Turn each sentence into an equation.
  3. For each equation make a small table of values: pick x, work out y. The dots on the graph are such whole-number points.
  4. Join each set of dots into a line. Where the lines cross is the answer. Check it in the story.

Move the guess point to try your own answers: it turns amber when it fits one equation (it is on that line) and green when it fits both.

🧮 Key formulas

a₁x + b₁y = c₁, a₂x + b₂y = c₂

Notebooks and pens: 2 notebooks + 3 pens = ₹110 gives 2x + 3y = 110; 4 notebooks + 1 pen = ₹170 gives 4x + y = 170.

ages: (x − 5) = 3(y − 5) → x − 3y = −10; (x + 10) = 2(y + 10) → x − 2y = 10

Five years ago both people were 5 years younger; in ten years both will be 10 years older.

boat: upstream speed = x − y, downstream speed = x + y, speed = distance ÷ time

30 km in 3 h upstream gives x − y = 10; 44 km in 2 h downstream gives x + y = 22.

fare = fixed charge + rate × distance: x + 10y = 105, x + 15y = 155

Simplified: all the stories are made up so the answers are whole numbers. The graph shows only positive values, because prices, ages and speeds cannot be negative.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • A shopkeeper checking two old bills for the price of each item.
  • Comparing two auto-rickshaw or taxi fare charts to see which is cheaper for your trip.
  • Boatmen on the Ganga or the Brahmaputra know a trip downstream is faster than the same trip upstream; the two times reveal the river's speed.
  • Mixing problems: how many ₹2 and ₹5 coins make ₹50 with 16 coins, or how much of two juices to mix for a set sweetness.

🎯 Try this

  1. In “Notebooks and pens”, drag the guess to the point where the two lines cross. What do a notebook and a pen cost? Check it in the story.
    Show answer(40, 10): a notebook ₹40 and a pen ₹10. Check: 2 × 40 + 3 × 10 = 110 ✓ and 4 × 40 + 10 = 170 ✓.
  2. Choose “Mother and son”. Put the guess at (35, 15). Which equation does it fit? Now slide along that line until the guess turns green.
    Show answerIt fits ① only: 35 − 3 × 15 = −10 ✓, but 35 − 2 × 15 = 5 ≠ 10. Sliding along line ① you reach (50, 20), where 50 − 2 × 20 = 10 ✓ fits ② too: the mother is 50 and the son 20.
  3. Choose “Boat on the Ganga” and switch off the lines. Can you find the answer just by thinking? Then switch them on to check.
    Show answerAdding the two equations gives 2x = 32, so x = 16 km/h, and then y = 6 km/h. Upstream the boat makes 16 − 6 = 10 km/h (30 km in 3 h) and downstream 22 km/h (44 km in 2 h).
  4. Choose “A bill that cannot be right”. Can the guess ever turn green? Why?
    Show answerNo. 4 pencils and 2 erasers are exactly twice 2 pencils and 1 eraser, so they should cost twice ₹14 = ₹28, not ₹30. The lines are parallel: there is no solution, so one of the bills must be wrong.
  5. Choose “The same fact told twice”. Find three different guesses that turn green. Why can't you find the prices?
    Show answerFor example (10, 3), (6, 5) and (4, 6): every point on the line fits both, because the second fact is just the first one doubled. With only one real fact, two unknowns cannot be pinned down.

📖 Key terms

  • Variable: a letter that stands for an unknown number, like the price of a pen.
  • Table of values: a few (x, y) pairs that satisfy an equation, used to draw its line.
  • Graphical method: solving a pair of equations by drawing both lines and reading where they cross.
  • Verification: putting the answer back into the original story to check that every fact is true.
