📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Not every three sticks make a triangle. Lay one on the ground and hinge the other two at its ends: their free ends can only meet if the two of them together are longer than the stick on the ground. If they are shorter, a gap is always left; if they are exactly equal, they only meet lying flat on the ground.

This is the triangle inequality: in every triangle, any two sides added together are longer than the third side. It also says that a straight line is the shortest way between two points: going via a third point is always longer.

🧮 Key rules

a + b > c, b + c > a, c + a > b

It is enough to check one: the two shorter sticks together must be longer than the longest stick.

|b − c| < a < b + c

The same thing written for one side: the third side must lie between the difference and the sum of the other two.

bigger side ⇔ bigger angle opposite it

In ΔABC, if BC > AC then ∠A > ∠B, and the other way round. The longest side faces the largest angle; the shortest side faces the smallest.

In the sim, stick AC swings down from A while stick BC always points at its free end. The distance from B to that end shrinks steadily from b + c to |b − c| (cosine rule), so the ends meet only if a lies between the two. The graph shows the gap between the ends; it reaches zero only for sticks that can make a triangle. Sticks are treated as lines with no thickness.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • If your home is 3 km from school and the shop is on the way only when it lies on the straight road, any detour through the shop makes the trip longer than 3 km. Walking across a park instead of along two sides of it is shorter for the same reason.
  • A mason checks whether three bamboo poles can form a support frame: if the two shorter ones together are not longer than the third, it will not work.
  • Mobile towers and GPS find your place from distances to known points; a set of distances that breaks the triangle inequality shows that a measurement is wrong.

📖 Key terms

  • Triangle inequality: the sum of any two sides of a triangle is greater than the third side.
  • Degenerate (flat) triangle: three points on one straight line; it has no area.
  • Opposite angle: the angle at the corner that a side does not touch.
