📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

A trapezium is a quadrilateral with one pair of parallel sides. Call them a and b, and call the distance between them (measured at right angles) the height h.

Make a copy of the trapezium and turn it upside down. Put it next to the first one and the two together make a parallelogram. Its base is a + b and its height is still h, so its area is (a + b) × h. Each trapezium is exactly half of it. You can also cut the trapezium along a diagonal into two triangles with the same height h: ½ × a × h + ½ × b × h gives the same answer.

Any quadrilateral can be split by a diagonal into two triangles. If the diagonal is d and the perpendiculars to it from the other two corners are h₁ and h₂, the area is ½ × d × (h₁ + h₂).

🧮 Formulas

Area of trapezium = ½ × (a + b) × h

Half the sum of the parallel sides, times the distance between them. The slanting sides are not needed at all.

Area of quadrilateral = ½ × d × (h₁ + h₂)

d is a diagonal that lies inside the shape; h₁ and h₂ are the perpendiculars to it from the other two corners.

1 hectare = 10 000 m² · 1 acre ≈ 4047 m²

Land is measured in square metres, hectares or acres.

Simplifications: lengths are whole centimetres and corners snap to the 1 cm grid, so the numbers stay simple. The field is drawn as a perfect trapezium on flat ground with straight boundaries; real fields are measured in parts by a surveyor and added up. The “check from the corners” in the quadrilateral view uses the coordinates of the corners (the shoelace method you will meet in Class 10).

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Farm fields and plots along a road or a canal often have two parallel sides. The patwari (land record officer) finds their area in square metres or acres from such measurements.
  • Embankments and canals: the cross-section of a canal or a railway embankment is a trapezium, so its area tells engineers how much soil to dig or fill.
  • Roofs and steps: the side face of a sloping roof, a staircase railing panel or a table top for a corner often has trapezium faces.

  1. In “copy and turn”, set a = 8 cm, b = 4 cm, h = 4 cm and press “Show the trick”. What is the area of the parallelogram, and of the trapezium?
    Show answerThe parallelogram has base 8 + 4 = 12 cm and height 4 cm, so 48 cm². The trapezium is half of it: 24 cm².
  2. Keep a, b and h, and move “Shift of the top side” from 0 to 8. Does the area change? Does the perimeter?
    Show answerThe area stays 24 cm², because it depends only on a, b and h. The perimeter changes, because the slanting sides get longer or shorter.
  3. Switch to “cut into two triangles” with the same sides. What are the areas of the two triangles?
    Show answer½ × 8 × 4 = 16 cm² and ½ × 4 × 4 = 8 cm². Together 24 cm², the same as the formula.
  4. Open “A field shaped like a trapezium”: parallel sides 120 m and 80 m, 50 m apart. Find its area in m² and in hectares.
    Show answer½ × (120 + 80) × 50 = 5000 m², which is 0.5 hectare (about 1.24 acres).
  5. In “Any quadrilateral”, the diagonal AC is 10 cm, h₁ = 3 cm and h₂ = 4 cm. What is the area? Now drag D sideways along its row of the grid. What happens?
    Show answer½ × 10 × (3 + 4) = 35 cm². Moving D sideways keeps its distance from AC, so h₂ and the area stay the same (as long as the shape does not fold over).

📖 Key terms

  • Trapezium: a quadrilateral with exactly one pair of parallel sides.
  • Height (of a trapezium): the perpendicular distance between the parallel sides.
  • Diagonal: a line joining two opposite corners of a quadrilateral.
  • Hectare: the area of a square 100 m by 100 m, 10 000 m².
