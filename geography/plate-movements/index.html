📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

The Earth's hard outer shell, the lithosphere (crust plus the top of the mantle, about 100 km thick), is broken into about seven large plates and many small ones. They ride on the soft, slowly creeping asthenosphere below and move a few centimetres a year, about as fast as your fingernails grow. Most mountains, rift valleys, volcanoes and earthquakes happen where two plates meet. There are three kinds of meeting place:

  • Convergent (coming together): if one plate is ocean floor, it is heavier and sinks under the other in a deep trench (subduction). Rock melts about 100 km down and feeds a row of volcanoes. If both are continents, neither sinks easily, so the crust crumples and piles up into fold mountains, like the Himalayas.
  • Divergent (pulling apart): the land stretches, cracks along faults and sinks into a rift valley. If it keeps opening, the sea floods in, and hot mantle rock rising in the gap makes new ocean floor at a mid-ocean ridge.
  • Transform (sliding past): crust is neither made nor destroyed, but the rocks stick, bend for a century or two, then slip suddenly in an earthquake.

India's story: India broke away from the old southern continent Gondwana and raced north across the Tethys Sea. The ocean floor in front of it sank under Asia. About 50 million years ago India hit Asia. Since then India has kept pushing north, its edge has slid under Tibet, and the squeezed rocks have risen into the Himalayas and the Tibetan Plateau. Rocks with sea fossils are found high in the Himalayas because they were once the floor of the Tethys Sea.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

distance = speed × time: 1 cm/yr × 1 million years = 10 km India: 5 cm/yr × 50 million years = 2,500 km pushed into Asia ocean depth ≈ 2.5 + 0.35 × √(age in million years) km a fault that stores 5 m of bend at 5 cm/yr slips about every 5 ÷ 0.05 = 100 years
  • New ocean floor is hot and high at the ridge. It cools, gets heavier and sinks as it moves away, so old floor lies deeper.
  • Molten rock at the ridge records the Earth's magnetic field as it cools. The field flips now and then, so the floor carries stripes of normal and reversed magnetism, the same on both sides of the ridge.
  • Under a subduction zone, earthquakes start shallow near the trench and get deeper further inland, along the sinking plate (yellow and blue rings in the sim).

Simplifications: heights are stretched many times more than depths; the plates move at one steady speed (India really moved about 15 cm a year before the crash and slowed to about 5 cm a year after it); mountain heights follow a simple formula with erosion built in; magnetic flips are drawn every 1–3 million years so the stripes are wide enough to see (real flips are irregular, often a few hundred thousand years apart); earthquake rings are only a sample of where quakes happen, not a count; in the map view the bending of the ground is drawn hundreds of times bigger than it is.

🌍 In real life

  • Himalayas: Mount Everest (8,849 m) still rises a few millimetres a year. The push of India causes big Himalayan earthquakes, such as Assam 1950 and Nepal 2015.
  • Andaman and Sumatra: the Indian plate sinks under the Burma plate. This made the 2004 earthquake and tsunami, and feeds Barren Island in the Andaman Sea, India's only active volcano.
  • Carlsberg Ridge in the Arabian Sea is a divergent boundary: the Indian and African/Somali plates move apart there. The East African Rift and the Red Sea show the first stages of an ocean being born; the Atlantic shows a grown-up one.
  • Transform faults: the Chaman Fault on the western edge of the Indian plate (Pakistan and Afghanistan), the Sagaing Fault in Myanmar and the San Andreas Fault in California. Roads, fences and streams that cross them are found shifted sideways.

📖 Key terms

  • Plate: a large slab of the lithosphere that moves as one piece.
  • Subduction: one plate sinking under another into the mantle; trench: the deep groove where it goes down.
  • Fold mountains: mountains made by squeezing and folding layers of rock (Himalayas, Alps).
  • Rift valley: a long valley that sinks between two faults as the land is pulled apart.
  • Elastic rebound: rocks bending under strain and snapping back in an earthquake.
