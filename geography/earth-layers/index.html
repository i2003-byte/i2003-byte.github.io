📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Nobody has ever been inside the Earth: the deepest hole ever drilled goes down only about 12 km, and the centre is 6,371 km away. We know what is inside from earthquake waves. They travel right through the planet, and the way they bend, speed up, slow down or stop tells us what they passed through.

The Earth has layers, like an egg: a thin rocky crust, a thick hot mantle of solid rock, a liquid outer core of molten iron and nickel, and a solid inner core. It gets hotter and denser the deeper you go.

🧮 Key ideas

P waves: solids and liquids · S waves: solids only

P (primary) waves push and pull the rock along their path, like sound, and are the fastest (6–14 km/s). S (secondary) waves shake it from side to side (3.5–7 km/s); a liquid cannot pass on a sideways shake.

S-wave shadow: S waves never arrive more than about 103° around the Earth from an earthquake. The whole far side gets none, because they cannot cross the liquid outer core. That is how we know the outer core is liquid.

P-wave shadow: P waves do cross the core, but they slow down sharply as they enter it and bend inwards, like light entering glass. So there is a ring between about 103° and 142° where hardly any P waves arrive.

Bending in the mantle: waves go faster deeper down, so their paths curve back up towards the surface.

The sim traces wave paths with Snell’s law through a simple version of the real Earth’s wave speeds. 🌍 Real-world examples

  • Seismographs in India (the National Center for Seismology runs more than 150 of them) pick up big earthquakes from all over the world, often 10–20 minutes after they happen.
  • A big earthquake in Japan or Chile is “in the shadow” for some stations: they get the P waves late or weak and no S waves at all.
  • The crust under the Himalayas is about 70 km thick, roughly twice as thick as under the Deccan plateau, and only about 7 km under the Indian Ocean floor.
  • The Earth’s magnetic field, which turns a compass needle, is made by liquid iron flowing in the outer core.

📖 Key terms

  • Crust: the thin, solid, outermost layer of the Earth.
  • Mantle: the thick layer of hot solid rock below the crust; its upper soft part is the asthenosphere.
  • Core: the iron-nickel centre of the Earth, liquid outside and solid inside.
  • Seismic waves: waves of shaking that spread out from an earthquake.
  • Shadow zone: the part of the Earth’s surface where certain earthquake waves do not arrive.
