📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

An earthquake starts at a point deep underground called the focus (or hypocentre). The spot on the surface right above it is the epicentre. From the focus, waves of shaking spread out in every direction, and seismographs far away record them as a wiggly line called a seismogram.

The waves do not arrive together. P waves are fastest and arrive first, with small quick wiggles. S waves are slower and come next, shaking harder. Surface waves run along the ground, arrive last and are the biggest; they do most of the damage. The farther away a station is, the bigger the gap between the P and S arrivals, just as you hear thunder later the farther away the lightning is.

🧮 Key ideas

distance = (S − P gap) × vp × vs / (vp − vs)

With P waves at 8.0 km/s and S waves at 4.5 km/s this is about 10.3 km for every second of gap. A gap of 60 s means the quake was about 620 km away.

Why it works: both waves cover the same distance d, so the S wave arrives d/4.5 − d/8.0 seconds after the P wave. That gap grows steadily with distance.

Three stations: one station tells you how far away the quake was, but not in which direction: it could be anywhere on a circle. Two circles meet at two points. A third circle picks the one true point. This is called trilateration; GPS finds your phone the same way, using distances to satellites.

The sim keeps it simple: waves travel in straight lines at fixed speeds (real waves curve and their speeds vary with depth and rock), the map is flat, and the shaking fades with distance. The outline of India is rough and the dates are of real earthquakes, but the traces are made up. 1 second of the sim is 20 seconds after the quake.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • The National Center for Seismology in New Delhi uses many stations across India to find each earthquake’s epicentre within minutes and shares it on its website and app.
  • The Bhuj earthquake of 26 January 2001 (magnitude 7.7) was felt as far as Delhi and Chennai; the Latur earthquake of 1993 struck an area long thought to be safe.
  • The Himalayan states and the North-East are in India’s highest earthquake zone because the Indian plate keeps pushing into Asia.
  • Some warning systems detect the fast P waves and send an alert before the damaging S and surface waves arrive, giving a few seconds to stop trains or shut gas lines.

📖 Key terms

  • Focus: the point underground where the rocks break and an earthquake starts.
  • Epicentre: the point on the surface right above the focus.
  • Seismograph: an instrument that records ground shaking; its record is a seismogram.
  • S − P interval: the time between the first P wave and the first S wave at a station.
  • Trilateration: finding a point from its distances to three known points.
