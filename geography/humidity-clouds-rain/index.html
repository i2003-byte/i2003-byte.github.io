📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Air always holds some invisible water vapour. The sun warms the sea and water evaporates into the air above it. How much vapour air can hold depends on its temperature: warm air can hold much more than cold air. Relative humidity tells you how full the air is, compared with the most it could hold at that temperature.

When moist air is pushed up a mountain, it expands and cools. At the dew point it is full (100% humidity), so the extra vapour condenses into tiny drops: a cloud. When the drops grow big enough they fall as rain. On the far side the air sinks, warms up and dries, so it rarely rains there: a rain shadow. Rivers carry the rainwater back to the sea, and the water cycle starts again.

🧮 Key rules

relative humidity = vapour in the air ÷ most it can hold × 100%

Air at 30 °C can hold about 27 g of vapour per kg of air; at 10 °C only about 8 g.

rising air cools ≈ 10 °C per km (no cloud) · ≈ 4–6 °C per km (in cloud)

Inside a cloud, condensing vapour gives out heat (latent heat), so the air cools more slowly. Sinking air warms by about 10 °C per km.

cloud base ≈ 125 m × (temperature − dew point)

A quick rule for air near the ground. If the air is 27 °C and its dew point 23 °C, clouds start about 500 m up.

Simplified: one bubble of air moves along the ground and is lifted only by the slope (real air also rises by convection); drops fall out once the cloud holds more than 0.3 g of water per kg of air; the rain depth assumes a 1 km thick layer of such air, so 1 g per kg rained out = 1 mm. Real monsoon rain comes from air flowing in for days, so totals are far larger. Heights are stretched to fit the picture.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Mumbai and the Konkan coast, on the windward side of the Western Ghats, get over 2,000 mm of rain a year; Pune, just behind the Ghats, gets about 700 mm.
  • On a humid day in Chennai or Kolkata, sweat does not evaporate easily, so you feel sticky and hot.
  • Dew on grass on winter mornings forms when the ground cools below the dew point of the air at night. A cold glass of water “sweats” for the same reason.

📖 Key terms

  • Humidity: the amount of water vapour in the air.
  • Dew point: the temperature to which air must cool to become saturated.
  • Condensation: vapour turning into liquid drops.
  • Windward / leeward: the side of a mountain facing the wind / the sheltered side away from it.
