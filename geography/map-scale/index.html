📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

A map is a small drawing of a big place. The scale tells you how much smaller: it links a length on the map to the real length on the ground. With it, a ruler on a school atlas can tell you how far it is from Delhi to Mumbai.

Scales are written in three ways, and they all say the same thing:

  • Statement scale: in words, “1 cm represents 100 km”.
  • Ratio scale or representative fraction (RF): 1 : 1,00,00,000. One unit on the map is 1,00,00,000 of the same units on the ground. It has no units, so it works in cm, inches or anything else.
  • Line (graphic) scale: a bar marked 0, 100, 200 … km. It stays correct even if the map is photocopied bigger or smaller, which is why it is used on screens.

A straight ruler gives the shortest distance (“as the crow flies”). Roads and railways bend, so to measure a route you lay a thread along it, then straighten the thread against the ruler. A map that shows a small area in detail (a village, 1 cm = 50 m) is a large-scale map; one that shows a whole country (1 cm = 100 km) is a small-scale map.

🧮 Key rules

real distance = map distance × scale

11.4 cm on a map where 1 cm represents 100 km is 11.4 × 100 = 1,140 km.

statement → RF: 1 km = 1,000 m = 1,00,000 cm, so 1 cm = 100 km becomes 1 : 1,00,00,000

Going back, cancel five zeros to turn cm into km: 1 : 50,00,000 means 1 cm represents 50 km.

map distance = real distance ÷ scale

India is 3,214 km from north to south. At 1 cm = 200 km it is 3214 ÷ 200 ≈ 16 cm long on the map.

Simplified: places are put on a flat map from their latitude and longitude, so distances are within a few per cent of the true distances over the curved Earth. The outline of India is rough. The picture is zoomed to fit your screen, so the ruler and the line scale use the map’s own centimetres, not your screen’s. Routes run straight between the towns named; real roads bend more, so a road trip is longer than the thread (New Delhi to Mumbai is about 1,400 km by road).

🌍 Real-world examples

  • School atlases print the scale of each map as an RF and a line scale. To fit India’s 3,214 km onto a page about 25 cm tall, a map of all India must be around 1 : 1,30,00,000. A map of one state fills the same page with a smaller area, so it has a larger scale.
  • Survey of India toposheets are large-scale maps: at 1 : 50,000, 2 cm on the sheet is 1 km on the ground, enough to show every village, well and footpath.
  • Google Maps and other phone maps show only a line scale, because when you pinch to zoom the map’s size changes and a fixed statement would be wrong.
  • Indian Railways gives fares by the route length in kilometres, which is longer than the straight-line distance between two stations.

📖 Key terms

  • Scale: the ratio between a distance on the map and the same distance on the ground.
  • Representative fraction (RF): the scale written as a ratio with no units, such as 1 : 50,000.
  • Large-scale map: shows a small area in great detail (for example 1 : 5,000 or 1 : 50,000).
  • Small-scale map: shows a large area like a country or the world with little detail.
  • Straight-line distance: the shortest distance between two places, measured with a ruler.
