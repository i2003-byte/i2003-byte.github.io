📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

The Earth goes round the Sun once in about 365¼ days. This is called revolution. The Earth's axis is tilted by about 23.5° and it always points the same way in space, towards the Pole Star. So around June the northern half of the Earth leans towards the Sun: the Sun climbs high at noon, its rays hit the ground more directly and the days are long. That is summer in India. Around December the northern half leans away: the Sun stays low, its rays spread out and the days are short. That is winter. The seasons are caused by the tilt, not by the distance from the Sun: the Earth is actually closest to the Sun in early January.

🧮 Key formulas & rules

latitude where the Sun is overhead: δ ≈ 23.5° × sin(360° × (284 + day number) ÷ 365) height of the Sun at noon = 90° − (your latitude − δ), ignoring the sign length of day: cos H = −tan(latitude) × tan δ, day = 2H ÷ 15 hours

δ goes from +23.5° (Tropic of Cancer, about 21 June) through 0° (equinoxes) to −23.5° (Tropic of Capricorn, about 22 December)

  • Sunshine falling on 1 m² of flat ground is largest when the Sun is overhead; it is proportional to sin(Sun's height).
  • The Earth–Sun distance changes only from about 147.1 to 152.1 million km, a change of about 3 %.

Simplification: the orbit picture is not to scale and the Earth is moved round it at an even speed (the real speed changes by about 3 %). Day lengths here ignore the bending of sunlight by air, which adds a few minutes. 🌍 In real life

  • The Tropic of Cancer (23.5° N) passes through eight Indian states, including Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh and West Bengal. Places on it have the Sun exactly overhead at noon around 21 June.
  • Makar Sankranti in mid-January marks the time when the Sun is seen moving north again after the shortest days.
  • When it is winter in India, it is summer in Australia: cricket fans watch Test matches in Sydney in the Indian winter.

  3. Set the tilt to 0° and let the year run. What happens to the graph and to the seasons?
    Show answerEvery place gets 12 hours of day on every date, and the noon Sun stays at the same height all year. With no tilt there would be no seasons.
  4. Pick Tromsø in Norway and look at 21 June and 22 December. What is special?
    Show answerOn 21 June the day lasts 24 hours (the midnight Sun), and on 22 December the Sun does not rise at all (polar night). Tromsø is north of the Arctic Circle.
  5. Pick Kanyakumari on 21 June. The Sun is 75° high at noon, but in which direction is it?
    Show answerTo the north! In June the Sun is overhead at the Tropic of Cancer, which is north of Kanyakumari, so the noon Sun is in the northern sky there.

📖 Key terms

  • Revolution: the movement of the Earth round the Sun, once a year.
  • Solstice: about 21 June and 22 December, when the Sun is overhead at a tropic and the day is longest or shortest.
  • Equinox: about 21 March and 23 September, when the Sun is overhead at the Equator and day and night are nearly equal everywhere.
  • Tropic of Cancer / Capricorn: the latitudes 23.5° N and 23.5° S, the farthest the overhead Sun reaches.
