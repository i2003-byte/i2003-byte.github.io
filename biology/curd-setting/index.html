📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

To set curd (dahi) at home, we warm boiled milk, stir in a spoon of old curd and leave the bowl covered for some hours. The spoon of curd is the starter: it carries millions of lactic acid bacteria (Lactobacillus), tiny rod-shaped microorganisms.

The bacteria feed on the sugar in milk (lactose) and turn it into lactic acid. They also multiply, so the more of them there are, the faster the acid builds up. As the milk turns more acidic its pH falls, and at about pH 4.6 the milk proteins (casein) join up into a soft net that traps the water. The runny milk has become solid curd.

🧮 Key ideas

lactose → lactic acid (by Lactobacillus)

C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁ + H₂O → 4 C₃H₆O₃. Fresh milk has a pH of about 6.6; set curd is about 4.5, sour curd about 4.

Warmth matters: the bacteria grow fastest at about 40 °C. In a warm place curd sets in 4–5 hours, overnight in a summer kitchen, but in a cold winter kitchen it may not set at all by morning. Milk that is still hot (55 °C or more) kills the starter.

Growth is fast: in warm milk the number of bacteria doubles about every 30–60 minutes, from 1 spoon of starter to about 100 times as many.

Stopping it: once set, curd is kept in the fridge. At 5 °C the bacteria almost stop, so it does not keep getting sourer. Left warm, the acid keeps rising, the curd turns sour and watery whey separates on top.

The sim uses a simple model: bacterial growth with a warmth factor that peaks at 40 °C, acid made in proportion to the bacteria, a brake as the milk gets very acidic, and pH read from a table for cow’s milk. The bowl stays at the temperature you choose. Real times depend on the milk, the starter and the weather. 1 second of the sim is 12 minutes in the bowl.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • In winter many families keep the curd bowl in a casserole, an oven with its light on or wrapped in a cloth near the stove, so it stays warm enough to set.
  • Dairies make curd and yoghurt in warm rooms at about 40–45 °C with pure starter cultures, so every cup sets the same way in a few hours.
  • The same kind of bacteria sour idli and dosa batter, and make buttermilk (chaas), paneer whey and cheese.
  • Curd bacteria are “friendly” microorganisms: they also help digestion, and the acid they make stops many harmful germs growing in the curd.

📖 Key terms

  • Lactobacillus: a rod-shaped bacterium that turns milk sugar into lactic acid.
  • Starter: a little curd that carries the bacteria into fresh milk.
  • pH: a scale of how acidic something is; below 7 is acidic, and the lower the number the more acidic.
  • Casein: the main protein in milk, which joins into a net when the milk becomes acidic.
  • Whey: the watery, pale yellow-green liquid that separates from sour curd or from paneer.
