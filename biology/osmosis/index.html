📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Osmosis is the movement of water through a selectively permeable membrane, from a region where there is more water (a weaker solution) to a region where there is less water (a stronger solution). Water molecules pass through the membrane easily; the dissolved salt or sugar is held back or passes very slowly.

Every cell membrane works like this. Put a cell in a hypotonic solution (weaker than its own sap, such as pure water) and water flows in, so the cell swells. In an isotonic solution there is no net flow. In a hypertonic solution (stronger, such as strong salt water) water flows out and the cell shrinks. A plant cell’s wall stops it bursting and makes it firm, or turgid. When it loses water, the membrane and cytoplasm pull away from the wall: this is plasmolysis.

🧮 Key formulas

water flows from weaker solution → stronger solution

“Stronger” means more dissolved particles per litre. Salt (NaCl) splits into two ions in water and its particles are light, so 1 g of salt pulls water about 12 times as strongly as 1 g of sugar. That is why a 10 % sugar solution affects the potato far less than 10 % salt.

% change in mass = (mass now − mass at start) ÷ mass at start × 100

Using the percentage change lets you compare strips or raisins of different sizes.

How the model works: water flow is set by the difference between the pull of the cell sap and of the solution, minus the push back from a stretched cell wall (or raisin skin). The constants are chosen to match typical school results: a potato strip gains about 8 % in water and loses about a quarter of its mass in strong salt; raisins nearly double. Time is speeded up. The model treats mass as proportional to volume and assumes no salt or sugar enters the cells; in real life a little does, over many hours.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Raisins (kishmish) soaked overnight in water swell up; this is how dried fruit is plumped for kheer.
  • Salt sprinkled on cut cucumber or mango for pickle draws water out, and the pieces go soft.
  • Wilted spinach or coriander perks up in cold water as its cells become turgid again.
  • Hospital drips use 0.9 % salt solution (“normal saline”) because it is isotonic with our blood cells; pure water would make red blood cells swell and burst.
  • Too much fertiliser near roots makes the soil water hypertonic, so roots lose water and the plant wilts.

🎯 Try this

  1. Soak the potato strip in pure water, then in 10 % salt. Compare the change in mass and the shape of the strip.
    Show answerIn water it gains about 8 % and stands up firm (turgid). In 10 % salt it loses about 27 % and flops over, because its cells lose water and become plasmolysed.
  2. Find the salt strength at which the potato strip hardly changes. What does this tell you?
    Show answerAbout 0.7 to 0.8 % salt. At this strength the solution is isotonic with the potato cells, so water enters and leaves at the same rate.
  3. Switch the solute to sugar and try 10 %. Why does the potato change so little compared with 10 % salt?
    Show answerA gram of sugar contains far fewer particles than a gram of salt (sugar molecules are heavy, and salt splits into two ions). Osmosis depends on the number of dissolved particles, so 10 % sugar is only about as strong as 0.9 % salt.
  4. Soak the raisins in pure water and in 10 % salt. Why do raisins still swell in 5 % salt, when the potato shrinks?
    Show answerA raisin’s cells are packed with sugar, so its inside is a very strong solution. Even 5 % salt is weaker, so water still flows in. Only at about 10 % salt is the outside as strong as the raisin.
  5. Look at Rhoeo leaf peel under the microscope in pure water, then in 5 % salt. What happens to the purple part of each cell?
    Show answerIn water it presses against the wall (turgid). In 5 % salt it shrinks and pulls away from the wall within a few minutes: plasmolysis. The cell wall keeps its shape, because it is freely permeable and stiff.

📖 Key terms

  • Selectively permeable membrane: lets some substances (water) through but not others.
  • Endosmosis / exosmosis: water moving into / out of a cell.
  • Turgid: swollen and firm with water, the membrane pressed against the wall.
  • Plasmolysis: the shrinking of the cytoplasm away from the cell wall when a plant cell loses water.
