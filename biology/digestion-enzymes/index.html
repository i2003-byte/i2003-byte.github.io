📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Food is made of big molecules: starch (long chains of glucose), proteins (chains of amino acids) and fats. They are far too big to pass through the wall of the gut into the blood. Digestion cuts them into small, soluble pieces: glucose, amino acids, and fatty acids with glycerol.

The cutting is done by enzymes, special proteins made by glands along the alimentary canal. Each enzyme works on one kind of food, and in the right conditions: pepsin in the acid of the stomach, the pancreatic enzymes in the alkaline small intestine. The small pieces are then absorbed by the villi of the small intestine into the blood.

🧮 Who does what

Mouth: starch —(salivary amylase)→ maltose

Chewing mixes food with saliva; only a little starch is digested here because the food soon leaves.

Stomach: protein —(pepsin + HCl)→ peptides

Hydrochloric acid (pH about 2) lets pepsin work and kills germs. Mucus protects the stomach wall from its own acid.

Small intestine: bile + pancreatic juice + intestinal juice

Bile from the liver (stored in the gall bladder) breaks fat into tiny droplets (emulsification) and makes the food alkaline. Pancreatic amylase, trypsin and lipase, and then the enzymes of the intestinal wall, finish the job: glucose, amino acids, fatty acids and glycerol.

Large intestine: water is absorbed

Undigested food such as fibre (roughage) is pushed out through the anus.

Simplifications: the times are typical but vary a lot from person to person (stomach 2–4 h, longer for oily food; small intestine 3–5 h; large intestine about a day). The rates are rounded, simple models. A little salivary amylase keeps working inside the swallowed lump for a while in the stomach; here it stops at once. The meals contain other things (fibre, vitamins, minerals, water) not drawn here, and the gut is shown unrolled and not to scale.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Chew a piece of chapati for a minute: it starts to taste sweet as amylase turns starch into maltose.
  • Acidity: too much stomach acid gives a burning feeling; antacids are mild bases (like magnesium hydroxide) that neutralise it.
  • Gallstones can block the bile duct. Then fat is digested poorly, and doctors advise a low-fat diet.
  • Diarrhoea is dangerous because water is not taken back in the large intestine; ORS (oral rehydration solution) replaces the lost water and salts.

📖 Key terms

  • Enzyme: a protein that speeds up one particular reaction without being used up.
  • Peristalsis: waves of muscle squeezing that push food along the gut.
  • Emulsification: breaking fat into tiny droplets, giving enzymes much more surface to work on.
  • Villi: finger-like folds of the small intestine wall that give a huge area for absorption.
