📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Every living thing is made of cells, and every cell comes from an earlier cell. A cell is the smallest part that can carry out all the jobs of life, so it is called the basic structural and functional unit of life. Robert Hooke first saw tiny boxes in a slice of cork in 1665 and named them cells.

Inside a cell, small parts called organelles share the work, like departments in a factory: the nucleus gives the instructions, mitochondria supply energy, ribosomes make proteins, and the Golgi apparatus packs and sends out products. Plant and animal cells have a true nucleus and organelles wrapped in membranes (they are eukaryotes). A bacterium is much smaller and simpler: its DNA lies loose in a nucleoid and it has no membrane-bound organelles (it is a prokaryote).

🧮 Key facts

1 µm (micrometre) = 1/1000 mm = 10⁻⁶ m

Most plant and animal cells are 10 to 100 µm across, so you need a microscope to see them. A bacterium is only 1 to 2 µm long. The scale bar under each drawing shows its real size.

plant cell = animal cell parts + cell wall + chloroplasts + one large vacuole

Plant cells have a stiff cellulose cell wall, chloroplasts (in green parts) and a large central vacuole. Animal cells have no wall, no chloroplasts, only small vacuoles, and more lysosomes.

How the model works: the cells are simplified drawings, not photographs. Real cells have many more mitochondria, ribosomes and ER tubes, and the parts are shown in flat outline. Each type is drawn at a different magnification to fill the screen; the scale bar tells you the true size.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • An onion peel under a school microscope shows brick-shaped plant cells with clear walls; a drop of iodine stains the nuclei.
  • Cells scraped gently from the inside of your cheek, stained with methylene blue, show round animal cells with no wall.
  • The bacteria that turn milk into curd (Lactobacillus) are prokaryotes: thousands of them would fit across one cheek cell.
  • Muscle cells have a lot of mitochondria because they need a lot of energy.

📖 Key terms

  • Organelle: a small part inside a cell with its own job.
  • Eukaryote: a cell with a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles (plants, animals, fungi).
  • Prokaryote: a cell with no true nucleus (bacteria).
  • Plastids: plant organelles such as chloroplasts (green) and leucoplasts (colourless, store starch).
