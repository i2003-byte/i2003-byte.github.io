📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Litmus only tells you whether a solution is acidic or basic. The pH scale tells you how acidic or basic it is, with a number from 0 to 14.

Below 7 is acidic (the lower, the more acidic), exactly 7 is neutral, and above 7 is basic (the higher, the more basic). Universal indicator is a mixture of several indicators, so it shows a different colour for each pH: red for strong acids, through orange and yellow, green at 7, then blue and purple for strong bases.

🧮 Key ideas and numbers

pH < 7 acidic · pH = 7 neutral · pH > 7 basic 1 step on the pH scale = 10 times more (or fewer) H⁺ ions

Acids give hydrogen ions (H⁺) in water and bases give hydroxide ions (OH⁻). pH 4 has 10 times more H⁺ than pH 5 and 1000 times more than pH 7.

Strong and weak: a strong acid such as the hydrochloric acid in your stomach gives up nearly all its H⁺, so it has a very low pH. A weak acid such as the acetic acid in vinegar gives up only a few, so the same amount has a higher pH.

Adding water spreads the ions out, so pH moves towards 7 but never crosses it: an acid stays acidic. Here a strong acid or base moves 1 pH unit for every 10 times more water, a weak one about half a unit, and baking soda solution stays close to 8.

Simplifications: the pH values are typical ones (real lemons, milk and soaps vary a little), the colours are a common universal-indicator chart, and the dilution rules are a simple model of what real solutions do.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Your stomach makes hydrochloric acid (pH about 1.5) to help digest food. Too much causes acidity; an antacid (a weak base) brings the pH up.
  • Tooth decay starts when bacteria in the mouth turn sugar into acid and the pH falls below about 5.5. Toothpaste is slightly basic.
  • Farmers test soil pH. Acidic soil is treated with quicklime or slaked lime; basic soil with organic matter such as compost.
  • Rain with a pH below 5.6 is called acid rain. It can damage buildings such as the Taj Mahal's marble.

📖 Key terms

  • pH: a number from 0 to 14 that tells how acidic or basic a solution is.
  • Universal indicator: a mixture of indicators that shows a different colour at each pH.
  • Strong acid: an acid that gives up almost all of its hydrogen ions in water.
  • Dilute: to add water so the solution becomes weaker.
