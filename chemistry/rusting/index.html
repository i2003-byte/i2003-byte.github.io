📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Iron left in moist air slowly turns into a flaky reddish-brown substance: rust. Rusting is a chemical reaction, a slow oxidation: iron combines with oxygen from the air, with water taking part. It needs both oxygen and water. Take away either one and the iron stays bright.

Rust is weak and crumbly, and it falls off, so fresh iron below is exposed and the damage keeps going. That is why we protect iron: by keeping air and water away (paint, oil, grease), by covering it with a more reactive metal (zinc in galvanising), or by mixing it with other metals to make an alloy such as stainless steel.

🧮 The reaction

4Fe + 3O₂ + 2xH₂O → 2Fe₂O₃·xH₂O (rust, hydrated iron(III) oxide)

The x means the amount of water in rust varies. The equation is balanced: 4 iron, 6 + 2x oxygen and 4x hydrogen on each side.

The same kind of slow oxidation is called corrosion for other metals (silver turns black, copper turns green). In food, oxidation of fats and oils makes them smell and taste bad: rancidity. Chips packets are filled with nitrogen and foods get antioxidants or airtight packing to slow it.

Simplified: one day passes per second, and the rusting speeds are chosen to show which tube rusts faster, not measured values. Real nails rust in patches that depend on the iron and the room. Tube C is an idealised “no air” tube; in a real one a little air dissolves again over many days.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Iron railings, gates and bridges are painted again every few years. Where the paint chips, rust starts.
  • Galvanised iron sheets, buckets and water pipes have a zinc coat. Even when it is scratched, the zinc corrodes first and saves the iron.
  • Cars, fishing boats and buildings near the sea in Mumbai, Chennai or Goa rust faster because of the salt in the air and spray.
  • Kitchen sinks, spoons and pressure-cooker parts are stainless steel, which does not rust.
  • The Iron Pillar in Delhi has stood for about 1600 years with very little rust. Its iron has a lot of phosphorus, which helps form a protective layer.

📖 Key terms

  • Rusting: the corrosion of iron by oxygen and water, forming hydrated iron(III) oxide.
  • Corrosion: a metal being slowly eaten away by substances around it, such as air, water and acids.
  • Galvanisation: coating iron or steel with a thin layer of zinc.
  • Alloy: a mixture of a metal with other metals or carbon (stainless steel = iron + chromium + nickel + a little carbon).
  • Rancidity: the spoiling of fats and oils in food by oxidation.
