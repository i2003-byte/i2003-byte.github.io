📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Everything around us is made of tiny particles that are always moving and attract each other. How tightly they are held decides whether a substance is a solid, a liquid or a gas.

  • Solid: particles are packed close in a fixed pattern and held strongly. They can only vibrate about their places, so a solid keeps its own shape and volume.
  • Liquid: particles are still close together, but held less strongly, so they can slide past one another. A liquid flows and takes the shape of its container, but keeps its volume.
  • Gas: particles are far apart and hardly attract each other. They fly about in all directions, fill the whole container and can easily be squeezed into a smaller space.

🧮 Key ideas

space between particles: solid < liquid ≪ gas hotter → particles move faster (more kinetic energy)

Each jar holds 100 mL. The solid takes 22 mL and the liquid 30 mL whatever the jar shape or the piston, while the gas takes all the room it is given.

Diffusion is the spreading of particles of one substance into another because of their own motion. It is fastest in gases, slow in liquids and almost zero in solids.

Simplifications: drawn in 2D and not to scale. In a real gas the particles are about 10 diameters apart and move at hundreds of metres per second (oxygen in air at 27 °C: about 480 m/s); here they are slowed down so you can follow them. The temperature slider only speeds the particles up; it does not melt or boil anything (see the heating curve simulation for that).

🌍 Real-world examples

  • The smell of hot food or agarbatti reaches you across the room quickly because the gas particles diffuse through the air.
  • A drop of ink spreads slowly in a glass of still water, and faster in hot water.
  • LPG and CNG are gases squeezed into strong cylinders; a lot of gas fits into a small space.
  • A brick keeps its shape on the ground, but water poured from a lota takes the shape of the bucket.

📖 Key terms

  • Matter: anything that has mass and takes up space.
  • Compressible: can be squeezed into a smaller volume (gases are; solids and liquids hardly are).
  • Diffusion: the mixing of particles of different substances on their own, because they move.
  • Fluid: something that can flow: liquids and gases.
