📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Atoms are most stable when their outer shell is full: 8 electrons (an octet), like the noble gases neon and argon. A metal atom has only 1, 2 or 3 electrons in its outer shell, so it is easier for it to give them away. A non-metal atom such as chlorine or oxygen is 1 or 2 electrons short, so it takes them.

The metal atom becomes a positive ion (cation), the non-metal atom a negative ion (anion). Opposite charges pull hard on each other. This pull is the ionic bond, and the compound is an ionic (electrovalent) compound.

🧮 Rules

Na (2,8,1) → Na⁺ (2,8) + e⁻ Cl (2,8,7) + e⁻ → Cl⁻ (2,8,8) Mg (2,8,2) + 2 Cl → Mg²⁺ + 2 Cl⁻ = MgCl₂

Electrons given = electrons taken, so the total charge of the compound is zero. Magnesium gives 2 electrons but each chlorine takes only 1, so it needs two chlorine atoms. Two aluminium atoms give 6 electrons, exactly what three oxygen atoms need: Al₂O₃.

Ionic solids melt at high temperatures because millions of ions hold one another in a lattice. Ions with bigger charges pull harder: Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ in MgO hold on far more strongly than Na⁺ and Cl⁻, so MgO melts at 2852 °C and NaCl at 801 °C.

To carry an electric current, charged particles must be free to move. In a solid the ions are locked in place, so it does not conduct. When it melts or dissolves, the ions move: positive ions to the negative electrode, negative ions to the positive one.

Simplified: atoms are drawn with the shell (Bohr) model, not to scale. The lattice is a flat 2D picture with only a few dozen ions; real crystals are 3D (NaCl is a cube pattern). At the electrodes the ions actually change into new substances (electrolysis); here a fresh ion simply enters from the far side. Melting points are rounded data-book values. MgO and Al₂O₃ hardly dissolve in water; CaO and Na₂O react with water, so their solutions hold OH⁻ ions instead of O²⁻.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Common salt is sodium chloride. Much of India's salt comes from sea water dried in the sun in Gujarat and Tamil Nadu, and from Sambhar lake in Rajasthan.
  • ORS (oral rehydration solution) has sodium chloride and potassium chloride. Their ions dissolved in water help the body hold water during diarrhoea.
  • Electric shocks are more dangerous with wet hands: the salts in sweat dissolve and their ions carry current.
  • Furnace linings and fire bricks use magnesium oxide and aluminium oxide because they melt only above 2000 °C.
  • Aluminium is made by passing current through molten aluminium oxide: the Al³⁺ ions collect at the negative electrode.

🎯 Try this

  1. Choose NaCl and press Start. How many electrons move, and what are the shells of the two ions?
    Show answerOne electron moves. Na⁺ has 2,8 (like neon) and Cl⁻ has 2,8,8 (like argon). Both now have full outer shells.
  2. Choose MgCl₂. Why are there two chlorine atoms?
    Show answerMagnesium (2,8,2) must lose 2 electrons, but a chlorine atom (2,8,7) can take only 1. Two chlorine atoms take one each, giving Mg²⁺ and two Cl⁻: total charge +2 − 2 = 0.
  3. Choose Al₂O₃ and count the electrons that move. Check the charges add up to zero.
    Show answerSix electrons move: each aluminium gives 3 and each oxygen takes 2. Charges: 2 × (+3) + 3 × (−2) = 0.
  4. Choose NaCl and “Test: solid in a bulb tester”, then “heat it until molten”, then “dissolve it in water”. When does the bulb glow, and why?
    Show answerNot for the solid: its ions are locked in the lattice. It glows once the salt melts (801 °C) and when it is dissolved, because then the ions are free to move to the electrodes.
  5. Now pick sugar and run the same three tests. What is different?
    Show answerThe bulb never glows. Sugar is covalent: it is made of molecules with no charge, so even when melted (at about 186 °C, a low temperature) or dissolved there are no ions to carry current.

📖 Key terms

  • Ion: an atom (or group of atoms) with a charge because it has lost or gained electrons.
  • Cation / anion: a positive ion (metals) / a negative ion (non-metals).
  • Octet: 8 electrons in the outer shell, a stable arrangement.
  • Electron-dot (Lewis) structure: the symbol with its outer electrons shown as dots or crosses.
  • Ionic (electrovalent) bond: the attraction between oppositely charged ions formed by electron transfer.
