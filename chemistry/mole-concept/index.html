📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Atoms are far too small and too many to count one by one. Chemists count them the way a shopkeeper counts coins: by weighing. A shopkeeper knows that 100 one-rupee coins weigh a fixed amount, so a heap that weighs ten times as much holds 1000 coins.

The chemist’s “bundle” is the mole. One mole is 6.022 × 10²³ particles, a number called the Avogadro constant. It was chosen so that one mole of a substance weighs its atomic or molecular mass in grams: 1 mole of carbon atoms (12 u each) weighs 12 g, and 1 mole of water molecules (18 u each) weighs 18 g.

So 12 g of carbon, 18 g of water and 58.5 g of salt look very different on a balance, but each holds exactly the same number of particles.

🧮 Key formulas

number of moles n = mass m ÷ molar mass M

The molar mass is the atomic, molecular or formula unit mass written in g/mol. For water, M = 2 × 1 + 16 = 18 g/mol, so 36 g of water is 2 mol.

number of particles N = n × 6.022 × 10²³

“Particles” means atoms for an element such as carbon or iron, molecules for water, sugar or oxygen gas, and formula units for ionic compounds such as salt (NaCl).

number of atoms = N × atoms in one particle

One water molecule has 3 atoms, so 1 mol of water holds 3 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 1.8 × 10²⁴ atoms. Sugar, C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁, has 45 atoms in every molecule.

Simplified: atomic masses are rounded (Cl = 35.5, Cu = 63.5, the rest whole numbers). The heaps and cubes use typical densities; the “one mole of each” cubes are drawn to scale, the heaps on the balance only roughly. Gas volumes are for 25 °C and normal air pressure, where 1 mol of any gas fills about 24 L. Weighing a real gas needs a sealed container; the balance here shows the mass of the gas alone.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • A glass of water (about 180 mL) is 10 mol of water: 6 × 10²⁴ molecules.
  • A 1 kg packet of sugar is 1000 ÷ 342 = 2.9 mol of sucrose: about 1.8 × 10²⁴ molecules.
  • An iron nail of 5.6 g is 0.1 mol of iron, still 6 × 10²² atoms.
  • Doctors and pharmacists measure some medicines and blood tests in millimoles per litre (mmol/L), for example blood sugar.
  • Fertiliser makers work out how many moles of nitrogen a bag of urea or ammonium sulphate gives to the soil.

📖 Key terms

  • Mole (mol): the amount of substance that contains 6.022 × 10²³ particles.
  • Avogadro constant (NA): 6.022 × 10²³ per mole, the number of particles in one mole.
  • Molar mass: the mass of one mole of a substance in grams per mole; numerically equal to its atomic, molecular or formula unit mass in u.
  • Atomic mass unit (u): one twelfth of the mass of a carbon-12 atom, about 1.66 × 10⁻²⁴ g.
  • Formula unit: the smallest repeating group of ions in an ionic compound, such as one Na⁺ with one Cl⁻ in salt.
