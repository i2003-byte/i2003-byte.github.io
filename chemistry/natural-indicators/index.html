📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Many things we eat and use are acidic (lemon juice, vinegar, curd) and taste sour. Others are basic (soap, baking soda, lime water) and feel soapy. Many are neither: they are neutral (sugar water, salt water, pure water).

Tasting or touching an unknown substance is dangerous, so we use an indicator: a substance that shows a different colour in an acid and in a base. Litmus comes from lichens, turmeric (haldi) from a root, and the china rose (gudhal, hibiscus) indicator from the petals of a flower. These are natural indicators.

🧮 The colour rules

blue litmus → red in an acid red litmus → blue in a base turmeric (yellow) → red-brown in a base china rose → dark pink in an acid, green in a base

A neutral solution changes none of them. One test is not always enough: if blue litmus stays blue, the solution is not acidic, but it could be basic or neutral. Test it with red litmus or turmeric to decide. The dots under each tube keep a record, and the label tells you once you can be sure.

Simplifications: colours are drawn as they usually look in a school lab; real shades depend on how strong the solution and the indicator are. Soap water is treated as basic, as most bathing and washing soaps are.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • A turmeric (haldi) stain on a white shirt turns red when you wash it with soap, because soap is basic. It turns yellow again when rinsed with plenty of water.
  • Ant and bee stings inject an acid; rubbing on a mild base such as baking soda paste helps.
  • Farmers test the soil: too acidic soil is treated with quicklime or slaked lime, a base.

🎯 Try this

  1. Test every tube with blue litmus. Which tubes turn it red?
    Show answerLemon juice and vinegar: they are acidic. The others keep it blue, so they are basic or neutral, and you cannot yet tell which.
  2. Now use red litmus. Which tubes turn it blue? Which tubes are now known to be neutral?
    Show answerSoap water, baking soda and lime water turn it blue (basic). Sugar water, salt water and distilled water changed neither litmus, so they are neutral.
  3. Can turmeric paper alone tell an acid from a neutral solution?
    Show answerNo. Turmeric stays yellow in both; it only changes (to red-brown) in a base.
  4. Which single indicator here can sort all three kinds in one go?
    Show answerChina rose: dark pink for acids, green for bases and no change for neutral solutions.
  5. Switch on “Mystery bottles”. Using as few tests as possible, sort bottles A to H into acidic, basic and neutral.
    Show answerChina rose alone does it in 8 tests. With papers only, test everything with blue litmus first, then use red litmus or turmeric only on the tubes that stayed blue.

📖 Key terms

  • Acid: a substance that tastes sour and turns blue litmus red.
  • Base: a substance that is bitter and soapy to touch and turns red litmus blue.
  • Neutral: neither acidic nor basic.
  • Indicator: a substance whose colour shows whether a solution is acidic, basic or neutral.
