📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Heat a beaker of ice steadily and watch its temperature. It does not rise all the time. It rises, then stays at 0 °C while the ice melts, rises again, then stays at 100 °C while the water boils. These flat parts are the most important part of the graph.

While ice melts, the heat goes into breaking the particles out of their fixed pattern, not into making them move faster. Temperature measures how fast the particles move, so it stays the same until all the ice has melted. The heat hidden in this way is called latent heat (latent means hidden). The same happens while water boils, when the particles are pulled right apart into steam.

🧮 Formulas and numbers

heat supplied Q = power × time = P t warming: Q = m c ΔT changing state: Q = m L

Specific heat c: ice 2100 J/(kg·°C), water 4186 J/(kg·°C). Latent heat L: melting 334 kJ/kg (fusion), boiling 2260 kJ/kg (vaporisation).

For 100 g of ice at −20 °C on a 500 W heater: warming the ice takes 4.2 kJ (8 s), melting 33.4 kJ (67 s), warming the water to 100 °C 41.9 kJ (84 s) and boiling it all away 226 kJ (7 min 32 s). Boiling needs almost 7 times more heat than melting.

Boiling point and height: water boils when its vapour can push back the air above it. High in the mountains the air pressure is lower, so water boils at a lower temperature: about 93 °C in Shimla and 88 °C in Leh.

Simplifications: all the heat goes into the water (none is lost to the air or the beaker), the water is stirred so it has one temperature, and the latent heat of boiling is taken as its 100 °C value at every height. The zoom window is a picture of the idea, not to scale. Time runs faster than real time; the readouts show the real heating time.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Ice in a glass of nimbu pani keeps the drink near 0 °C until it has all melted.
  • Steam at 100 °C burns more than boiling water at 100 °C: as it condenses on the skin it gives out its large latent heat.
  • Dal takes much longer to cook in Leh, because the water boils at only about 88 °C. A pressure cooker raises the boiling point to about 120 °C, so food cooks faster.

📖 Key terms

  • Melting point: the temperature at which a solid turns into a liquid (ice: 0 °C).
  • Boiling point: the temperature at which a liquid boils all through (water: 100 °C at sea level).
  • Latent heat: heat taken in or given out when a substance changes state, without any change in temperature.
  • Specific heat: the heat needed to warm 1 kg of a substance by 1 °C.
