📘 Learn the science

💡 The big idea

Particles in a liquid move at different speeds. A few fast ones near the surface have enough energy to break away from the others and escape into the air as vapour. This change of a liquid into vapour below its boiling point is called evaporation. It happens only at the surface.

The particles that escape are the most energetic ones, so the water left behind has less energy on average: it gets cooler. It keeps cooling until the heat flowing in from the warmer air and the dish balances the heat carried away by evaporation.

🧮 What makes water evaporate faster

larger surface area → faster higher temperature → faster lower humidity (drier air) → faster more wind → faster

The model: rate = (transfer coefficient) × (surface area) × (vapour the air could hold at the water's temperature − vapour already in the air). Wind increases the transfer coefficient by sweeping away the damp air above the water.

Every gram of water that evaporates takes about 2430 J (its latent heat of vaporisation) away from the water that is left.

Simplifications: indoors in the shade (sunshine would add much more heat), 100 g of water stirred so it has one temperature, and the air in the room stays at the same temperature and humidity. One second on screen is 20 minutes.

🌍 Real-world examples

  • Water in an earthen pot (matka or surahi) stays cool in summer: it seeps through tiny pores and evaporates from the whole outer surface.
  • Sweating cools us. On a humid day in Mumbai sweat does not evaporate easily, so we feel sticky and hot; in dry Delhi heat it evaporates quickly.
  • Clothes dry faster on a sunny, windy day, and faster when spread out than when bunched up.
  • In summer, people sprinkle water on the floor or courtyard to cool it, and desert coolers blow air through wet pads.

    Show answerThe glass's surface is only 7 cm across instead of 20 cm, about 8 times less area, so only about 0.7 g/h evaporates. Evaporation happens only at the surface.
  4. Set the humidity to Humid (Mumbai, July). What happens?
    Show answerAt 85% humidity the air already holds most of the vapour it can, so evaporation drops to about 0.6 g/h and the water cools only about 2 °C below the air.
  5. At 100% humidity, does the water still cool?
    Show answerHardly at all: the air cannot take any more vapour at its temperature, so evaporation stops once the water is as warm as the air.

📖 Key terms

  • Evaporation: a liquid changing into vapour at its surface, at any temperature below its boiling point.
  • Humidity: the amount of water vapour in the air; 100% means the air holds all it can at that temperature.
  • Latent heat of vaporisation: the heat needed to turn 1 kg of liquid into vapour without changing its temperature.
